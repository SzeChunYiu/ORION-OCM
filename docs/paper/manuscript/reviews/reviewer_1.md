# Reviewer 1 (validity, methods, data, inference)

Written from scratch against manuscript draft V3 and the receipt-bound files it cites; blind to the other reports. Lens: does the design permit the inferences, are the statistical units right, are the numbers what the receipts say. Recommendation posture: major revision, with the central inference partly established.

## Overall assessment

The paper is unusually explicit about its evidence boundaries, and the receipt chain plus the claim map let a reviewer check every number. The primary inferential result (a one-sided exact sign test over eight paired lifetimes on the conversations family, p = 0.0039, replicated byte-identically) is correctly specified and correctly bounded: the pre-registration names one primary family, the size (9/256) and power (0.81 at a per-lifetime win probability of 0.9) are exact, the six secondary families are correctly flagged as one coin, and the manuscript does not let them carry inferential weight. The main validity problems are not in the statistics but in what the comparison can mean: the parent has no learned component, the streams are substitutions of one authored world, and after the receipts were frozen the runtime's adoption gate was found defective, which reopens every self-repair cell. The manuscript says all of this; the question for the editor is whether what survives is enough.

## Major concerns

**R1-1 (blocking, claim recalibration). The primary family's variation across streams is thin.** Table 8 says the primary family is "not collapsed" because parent means vary (0.593 to 0.611). The receipt shows the parent varies over only two values (0.6111 in six lifetimes, 0.5926 in two), so the eight lifetime differences take exactly two values, 0.3704 and 0.3889. The sign test remains valid (the sign is what is tested), but the manuscript's contrast between the primary family ("varies") and the secondaries ("collapsed") overstates how much independent variation the streams inject. Resolution test: state the two-value structure in Section 5.11 and in the Table 8 row, and say that the substitution generator varies the conversations family only through the two parent scores; the collapse flag as pre-registered is a binary condition and the family passes it, but readers should see how narrowly.

**R1-2 (blocking, must address). Self-repair results after the revalidation.** Sections 5.8, 5.9, 5.11 and 9 report that the adoption gate keyed its component table by the machine and never checked the exact predecessor of the replaced layer, that the seven M11 cells and the three M12 V2 phase-G cells are reopened, and that the programme now labels M11 and M12 as requiring protected re-evaluation. The manuscript nevertheless keeps "rolled back exactly in 7 of 7" and "repaired, preserved and rolled back exactly in 8 of 8" in the results voice. A reader could take the engineering replay ("reproduced the descriptive summary") as a renewal. Resolution test: every self-repair count in Sections 5.8, 5.9, 5.10 and 5.11 and in the abstract should read as a historical receipt value under a reopened gate, with one sentence in each place saying what the reopened gate did not check; Table 5's last row does this well and the prose should match it. If the authors can run the protected re-evaluation before submission, that replaces the caveat with a result.

**R1-3 (major, must address). The cross-domain transfer family compares unequal cell sets.** Table 6 reports 6 of 6 machine cells against 0 of 4 parent cells, and the V4 secondary family E is the same construction at the lifetime level (machine 1.000, parent 0.000). The manuscript now says the current evaluation returns that comparison as undetermined and that matched cells are an open obligation. Good. But Table 8 still lists E as a rejecting secondary family. Resolution test: mark the E row in Table 8 as "rejects on unequal cell sets; undetermined as a comparison" in the verdict column, so that the table is not more favourable than the text.

**R1-4 (major). Two graders disagree on the reference arm's honest unknowns.** Section 6 reports 7 honest unknowns from the summary grader and 237 assertions from the four-class grader over the same 240 items, and correctly notes the four-item disagreement in two streams. This is a measurement-validity finding about the grading pipeline, not a footnote. Resolution test: name which grader the Table 9 row uses (it uses the summary grader), state in the caption that the four-class grader implies 3 licensed unknowns, and commit to the four-class grader as the pre-registered one for V5, or explain why the summary grader is retained.

**R1-5 (major). Item exchangeability in the n = 54 family.** The M7 and V2 conversations residual treats 54 turns from 12 conversations as exchangeable items; batch 6 (F2) gives exact sizes under block dependence, and the manuscript carries the caveat. The caveat should be quantified once: at block size 6 (the natural conversation block) the item-level test's exact size is 65/256, not 0.05. Resolution test: report that size in Section 4 or 5.4 so a reader knows the V2 turn-level p-value is not an α = 0.05 statement under conversation-level dependence; the V3 and V4 lifetime-level tests do not have this problem and the manuscript should say that they were built to remove it.

**R1-6 (major). Minimum-n rule and the descriptive families.** The 40-item minimum is pre-registered and followed, and every sub-40 family is labelled descriptive. However Table 3 labels several sub-40 families "inconclusive" in the verdict column and "CANNOT_CHECK (n < 40)" in the terminal column; "inconclusive" is a test outcome word and could be read as a completed test. Resolution test: use one word for one state (the receipt's own verdict field is INCONCLUSIVE, so keep it, but define it once as "test not run to decision because n < 40").

## Minor comments

- Table 3: the ci_90 for the conversations family in the receipt is [0.2673, 0.3704]; the manuscript prints "0.267 to 0.370" and "difference +0.370". A 90% interval whose upper end equals the point estimate is unusual; explain in the caption that the interval is the Clopper–Pearson interval of the discordant proportion mapped to the rate-difference scale, which is one-sided at the top when all discordant pairs favour one arm.
- Section 5.1: "agrees with the exact solver to 10⁻¹⁴" is from the ledger's backlog table; the scaling baseline JSON carries `sparse_vs_exact_max_error = null` for the rows shown. Cite the ledger, not the baseline, for that number, or drop it.
- Section 5.7: "1.28 against an oracle of 0.5" rounds 1.278; fine, but the same paragraph reports −0.49 for −0.488; state the rounding rule once (two decimals) in the Methods summary.
- Section 6: "the reference answered every out-of-scope question from pretraining" is a claim about the source of the answers; the receipt establishes only that the answers were assertions on unknown-licensed items. Write "asserted an answer to every out-of-scope question" and let the pretraining attribution be the interpretation it is.
- Methods: the phrase "reproduced by the theory batches' checkers" should name which tests were reproduced (D1 sizes, F2 block sizes, G8 sizes and powers).

## What is assessable and what is not

Assessable from the supplied material: every number in Tables 1 to 10 (the claim map resolves 239 of 241 rows by machine; the two note-only derivations are arithmetic on receipt fields). Not assessable: whether the protected re-evaluation of the self-repair cells will reproduce the historical values; whether the reference model's answers came from pretraining rather than from the prompt (the design cannot separate these, which is why the arm is a reference).
