    Full family matrix: exact search is admissible everywhere, and flatness tracks target-agnosticism
    
    All nine hand-built rows across six ecologies, minimum over the six interventions. Both
    exact-search rows are admissible on every ecology at mean 0.9653; memory on three,
    retrieval on one, nothing else anywhere. The cost-biased ranking corrected above is
    confirmed wrong.
    
    The flat-ceiling hypothesis turns out half right, which is more interesting than either
    verdict I gave it. It fails universally, since most families vary as much as the constant.
    But it holds sharply for the one family that is universally admissible: both search rows
    sit at stdev 0.0260, half the constant's 0.0516, with the smallest spread of any row.
    
    That is mechanistic rather than coincidental. An exact search over the grammar is
    target-agnostic, so its capability barely depends on which ecology it faces, while a fixed
    constant is one value against varying targets and shows the largest spread in the table.
    Flatness tracks target-agnosticism, and the flattest family is admissible everywhere.
    
    Recorded alongside it, the capability/reachability gap with both numbers: the exact-search
    family is admissible on 6 of 6 ecologies but the class-rate record recovers the program
    class on 1 of 3 seeds. That gap is not a defect in either measurement, it is DU-1 --
    admissibility is a property of the static package and reachability of the development law.
    The class-rate P-prime clause already couples them empirically; this supplies the
    capability half with numbers.
