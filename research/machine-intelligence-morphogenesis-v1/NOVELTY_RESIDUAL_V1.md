## Candidate surviving scientific object

Let `B` be a candidate adaptive generating basis; let `M` be a complete machine-intelligence morphology; let `E` be a task ecology including resource prices/budgets and verification conditions.

The surviving Track-B question is whether there exists a compact basis or equivalence class of bases such that:

1. **Cross-paradigm bounded derivation** — major known morphologies can be compiled from `B` while preserving registered behavior **and developmental update behavior** under bounded resource inflation.
2. **Nontrivial minimality** — removing a primitive after allowing compensating composition causes a registered algebraic/learning/resource loss somewhere; redundant syntactic bases quotient together.
3. **Developmental acquisition** — architecture-specific structures can be acquired from experience/search without being directly named in the primitive vocabulary.
4. **Prospective phase prediction** — measurable coordinates of `E` predict which morphology family or Pareto region should be favored before architecture search sees protected outcomes.
5. **Blind recovery** — neutral search from the same basis recovers predicted known morphology classes more often/cheaply than incompatible classes in the registered regimes.
6. **Novel morphology test** — a proposed new form counts only if it is not bounded-equivalent to known forms and occupies a reproducible new capability/resource/developmental region.

## Strongest possible upward claim

A future high-level result would have the form:

> Under explicit computability and resource assumptions, several known machine-intelligence paradigms are resource-bounded specializations of a common adaptive generating basis. A theory derived from the basis predicts morphology transitions as ecology/resource/verification parameters vary, and blind architecture search prospectively recovers those transitions on disjoint task families.

That would still **not** prove one metaphysically unique atom of intelligence or a universally optimal architecture.

## Why this would be more than universal computation

A universal machine can emulate everything but normally gives no useful answer to:

```text
Which representation should be learned?
Which topology should appear?
Which update law should dominate?
When should explicit rules beat parametric weights?
When should probabilistic inference beat deterministic programs?
When should a hybrid dominate both?
How much development/search is required to reach each form?
How does the answer change with noise, compositionality, verification strength,
reuse horizon, drift, interaction cost or resource prices?
```

Track B only survives if it yields falsifiable answers to questions of this type.

## Candidate qualitative predictions to harden later

These are **not yet registered confirmatory predictions**:

- high-volume noisy smooth data + high repeated-inference horizon may favor amortized parametric/neural forms;
- sparse exact compositional structure + strong formal verification may favor explicit programmatic/symbolic forms;
- high uncertainty + expensive observations may favor probabilistic belief/inference forms;
- mixed statistical perception plus exact compositional verification may favor hybrid forms;
- frequent local revision/revocation may penalize tightly entangled representations unless their update cost remains low;
- rapidly shifting ecologies may favor forms with cheap adaptation even when their steady-state inference is less efficient.

The next theory pass must derive or delete these from explicit assumptions rather than preserve them as intuition.
