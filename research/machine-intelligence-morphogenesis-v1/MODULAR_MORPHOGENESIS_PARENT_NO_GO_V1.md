# Modularity / hierarchy morphogenesis parent no-go v1

Status: **direct parent subtraction against a weak Track-B morphology-phase claim.**

## Claim attacked

A tempting headline for Track B is:

> task/ecology/resource structure causes a particular machine architecture to emerge, and architectures that match repeated substructure become more evolvable.

That broad phenomenon is already strongly parent-owned.

## Modularly varying goals

Kashtan & Alon evolved Boolean circuits and neural networks under goals that periodically changed while preserving/recombining common subproblems. They reported:

```text
fixed goal                         -> typically compact/nonmodular solutions
randomly varying unrelated goals   -> typically nonmodular; slow adaptation
modularly varying related goals    -> spontaneous modular architecture/motifs;
                                     much faster adaptation after goal changes
```

The evolved networks discover shared subproblems and instantiate them as reusable modules; later goals can be reached by comparatively small rewiring/parameter changes.

Primary anchors:

- Kashtan & Alon, **Spontaneous evolution of modularity and network motifs**, PNAS 102(39), 2005, DOI `10.1073/pnas.0503610102`, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1236541/
- Kashtan, Noor & Alon, **Varying environments can speed up evolution**, PNAS 104(34), 2007, DOI `10.1073/pnas.0611630104`, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1948871/
- Parter, Kashtan & Alon, **Facilitated variation: how evolution learns from past environments to generalize to new environments**, PLoS Computational Biology / related analytic MVG work; see the parent literature around modularly varying goals.

An analytically solvable model later reproduced rapid emergence of modular structure under modularly varying goals:

- Kashtan et al., **An Analytically Solvable Model for Rapid Evolution of Modular Structure**, PLoS Computational Biology 5(4), 2009, DOI `10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000355`, https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000355

## Resource pressure can also generate modularity

Clune, Mouret & Lipson showed that adding pressure to minimize network connection costs alongside performance produces networks that are substantially more modular and more evolvable than performance-only controls, even without modularly varying goals.

- Clune, Mouret & Lipson, **The evolutionary origins of modularity**, Proceedings of the Royal Society B 280, 2013, DOI `10.1098/rspb.2012.2863`, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3574393/

Subsequent work showed connection costs can also promote hierarchy, another architecture-level organization rather than merely local parameter change.

## What these parents already own

Track B gets no novelty credit for the generic statement:

```text
ecological regularities / changing related tasks / resource pressures
-> architectural organization such as modularity or hierarchy
-> improved future adaptability/evolvability
```

They are especially close to the user's original Track-B idea because:

- architecture is not manually fixed;
- the same low-level evolutionary substrate generates different network organizations;
- task variation/resource regime changes which structure emerges;
- the resulting architecture changes future adaptation speed.

This is already a bona fide morphology-phase phenomenon.

## Why Track B may still survive

The parent results mainly concern specific search processes and architecture families (evolved circuits/neural networks) and specific morphology statistics such as modularity/hierarchy.

The surviving Track-B bar is stronger:

1. **cross-paradigm:** the same predictive structural theory must cover neural, programmatic, probabilistic, symbolic/OCM-like families rather than only evolved networks;
2. **quantitative prospective phase law:** freeze morphology/frontier predictions before protected architecture search;
3. **developmental equivalence/resources:** compare full acquisition/update/inference/retention/verification costs, not only modularity score/evolution generations;
4. **strong parent product:** algorithm selection, modularity/evolvability, AutoML, meta-learning, resource rationality and family-native predictors all receive first refusal;
5. **unknown-form prediction:** ideally predict a phase region requiring a structure not already in the parent atlas, then discover it blindly.

## A likely cross-paradigm motif: reusable factorization

The parent studies strengthen, rather than originate, one candidate general principle:

> if a task ecology repeatedly recombines partially independent subproblems, a factorization aligned with those recurring dependencies can reduce future reconfiguration/search cost.

But this principle overlaps strongly with:

```text
modularity/evolvability literature
factored MDPs and graphical models
library learning/program abstraction
hierarchical RL/options
mixture-of-experts/modular networks
software modularity
```

So `reusable modular decomposition` is not itself an ORION novelty.

## Decisive residual

A stronger result would derive a **pre-outcome dependency/coupling signature** of an ecology and predict the best factorization class under full resource/developmental accounting across multiple paradigms.

Candidate research question:

\[

\to
\text{predicted developmental factorization frontier}.
\]

Then blind search from a neutral basis must recover the predicted factorization on held-out ecologies.
