# 2. Q1 — “What is the set / unique form of the basic cognitive unit?”

## 2.1 Strong answer

There is currently **no representation-independent unique local cognitive atom** established by computation alone.

A component can often be split into smaller transformations or merged with neighbors while preserving the registered external computation. Universal programming formalisms also allow many mutually compiling bases. Therefore a unit boundary is not invariant unless extra physical, causal, locality, resource or verification assumptions are declared.

The notebook intuition was directionally useful but the object was one level too low.

## 2.2 The replacement object

Fix the registered obligation, development protocol and future interventions. Let `d` and `d'` be complete developmental situations. Define

\[
d \sim^{sem}_{\Omega,D,J} d'
\]

iff every registered future intervention induces the same probability law over protected future semantic traces.

Then

\[
z^{sem}=[d]_{\sim^{sem}_{\Omega,D,J}}
\]

is the **semantic developmental sufficient state**.

At finite deterministic scope this is the strongest current representation-independent minimal-state object. It is task-relative, future-sensitive and developmental. It is **not** a neuron, rule, token, OCM asset, instruction or universal physical atom.

## 2.3 What can still count as a useful local unit?

A local factor may be called minimal only relative to a registered realization class and resource/compiler contract. A practical criterion is:

> a factor is locally irreducible at scope if no allowed split/merge/refactoring preserves the registered semantic/developmental behavior while improving the declared total realization burden beyond the allowed compiler overhead.

This creates **resource-relative minimal adaptive factors**, potentially many non-isomorphic ones. Therefore the answer to “what is the set?” is generally an equivalence class / Pareto set, not one globally unique syntax.

# 4. Q3 — “What limits a form of intelligence? Cost? Why can neural networks dominate? Does d(development)/d(cost) -> infinity?”

This question is now one of the strongest parts of the formal synthesis.

## 4.1 Primary object: developmental frontier

For morphology `M`, ecology `E`, history `H` and complete resource vector `b`, define verified reachable capability vector `Q(M,E,H,b)` and

\[
\mathcal F_M(E,H)
=\operatorname{Pareto}\{(b,Q):Q\text{ legally reachable by }M\}.
\]

A morphology is not “more intelligent” merely because one benchmark score is higher. It dominates only relative to the registered capability obligations and resource/risk coordinates.

## 4.2 The notebook derivative

After prospectively freezing a scalar capability/utility coordinate and price vector, let `c` be scalarized cost and `Q_M^*(c)` the best verified capability reachable under that cost. Then

\[
\boxed{
\eta_M(c)=\frac{\partial Q_M^*(c)}{\partial c}
}
\]

or the corresponding finite difference is the precise version of the notebook’s

```text
d(development) / d(cost).
```

The Pareto frontier remains primary because scalarization can hide tradeoffs.

## 4.3 Why the ratio does not generally go to infinity

A literal universal limit

\[
\frac{d\,development}{d\,cost}\to\infty
\]

is not supported.

Three distinctions matter:

1. **amortization:** a large fixed acquisition/build cost can be spread over many future uses, so average cost per use may fall sharply;
2. **local phase change:** a finite structural change can unlock a large capability jump, making a local discrete ratio large;
3. **total lifecycle burden:** information, search, update, verification, storage, maintenance, revision, hardware/energy and failure costs do not disappear merely because inference becomes cheap.

An apparent infinite derivative can also be a bad scalarization artifact when the denominator approaches zero while omitted resource coordinates remain nonzero. The correct theory-level statement is frontier movement under complete resource accounting, not “free intelligence.”

## 4.4 Six distinct ceilings

A plateau can come from different mechanisms and should not be called one generic intelligence limit:

1. **representation/expressivity ceiling** — required distinctions/operators are outside bounded reach;
2. **search/update ceiling** — the solution is representable but prohibitively hard to find or acquire;
3. **plasticity ceiling** — fresh learning becomes progressively harder as history accumulates;
4. **stability/retention ceiling** — new learning damages old competence or causes negative transfer;
5. **verification/governance ceiling** — useful internal outputs cannot be externally certified/adopted under the required evidence contract;
6. **morphogenesis/discovery ceiling** — a better realization exists but finite `Gamma` cannot reliably discover it within budget.

The 2024 deep continual-learning plasticity results are directly relevant to item 3: standard deep learning can lose plasticity over long task sequences, showing that strong static/short-horizon performance is not identical to indefinite developmental capacity.

## 4.5 Why neural networks can dominate a large present-day region

Neural morphology need not be fundamental to dominate a regime. Current parent-grounded reasons include:

```text
differentiable credit assignment
shared/distributed representations
large-data statistical generalization
training-to-inference amortization
highly optimized dense tensor kernels
accelerator/software co-design
pretraining that creates broadly useful priors
```

Compute-optimal scaling results such as Chinchilla also show that even **within** the neural family the efficient frontier depends on the allocation of model size, data and compute. Neural dominance is therefore a phase-region statement, not a universal theorem.

The frontier can plausibly move toward explicit/programmatic/probabilistic/hybrid forms when exact verification, sparse compositional structure, uncertainty, local revision, provenance, long-lived continual plasticity, low-data acquisition or different hardware/resource prices become dominant constraints.

# 5. The “language -> math -> AGI?” branch in the notebook

Language and mathematics are important ecologies; neither is by itself a definition of general intelligence.

A stronger developmental criterion is whether one persistent system can, under bounded resources and fixed external verification/authority rules:

```text
acquire new representations/operators
transfer useful structure to fresh task families
change pre-solution proposal/search geometry
recognize representation/method failure
revise or replace methods
retain old competence while learning new competence
lower future acquisition burden (K2 candidate)
possibly improve the process that finds future improvements (K3 candidate)
```

Therefore success in language or mathematics is evidence only to the extent that the same developmental semantics survive disjoint domains and produce verified transfer/frontier improvement without hidden architecture forks or uncharged external engineering.

A reasonable operational GMI target is not “can solve language and math,” but:

> **does one resource-bounded developmental system beneficially expand its verified capability-resource frontier across a declared heterogeneous ecology family while preserving external verification, retention and honest accounting?**

This is why Track B’s E3/E4 validation programme matters.

# 9. Falsifiable predictions implied by the current synthesis

These are theory-guided predictions, not all claimed as ORION novelties.

### P1 — regime reversals

Architecture rankings should reverse when ecology structure, verification demands, horizon or physical resource prices move far enough. A universal winner over unrestricted ecologies is not expected.

### P2 — reuse-amortization threshold

A more expensive reusable morphology should become favorable only after its future bias/coordination savings repay build, maintenance and verification costs.

### P3 — plasticity is a separate frontier coordinate

Strong initial neural performance should not guarantee strong long-horizon acquisition productivity; sustained-learning regimes can expose loss of plasticity even when static expressivity remains high.

### P4 — factorization follows dependency economics, not labels

When task dependencies become more modular and cross-module coordination is costly, matching modular factorizations should move toward the frontier; when cross-module interaction dominates and horizon is long, more integrated realizations may recover advantage.

### P5 — matched developmental history should alter pre-solution geometry

For related ecologies, useful history should move probability/rank toward future admissible candidates before solution access; unrelated or mismatched history should show weaker benefit or negative transfer.

### P6 — a genuine unknown-form prediction must precede search

If a supposedly new morphology is proposed before a frozen parent-frontier hole/property vector exists, it does not count as GMI theory-predicted discovery.

Failures of these predictions at registered scope should narrow or kill the corresponding theory layer rather than be explained away post hoc.
