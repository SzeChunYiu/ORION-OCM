# Q3 — What limits a form of intelligence, and why does one form dominate?

## Current answer: developmental geometry + lifecycle economics give the right shape, but the general predictive map is open

A morphology is usefully characterized by a developmental geometry

\[
\mathcal G_M=(\mathcal H_M,\pi_M,K_M,c_M,V_M)
\]

plus retention/plasticity behavior.

Interpretation:

```text
H_M  what configurations/hypotheses are reachable
pi_M what useful configurations receive proposal/search mass before evidence
K_M  how experience changes the machine
c_M  what resources transitions consume
V_M  what feedback/verification is available
```

These ingredients are parent-heavy. At finite explicit scope they reduce to standard sequential decision / stochastic-shortest-path / metareasoning formalisms.

### Exact idealized phase theorem

For target ecology distribution `P`, morphology proposal prior `Q_M`, build cost `B_M` and lifetime horizon `N`, under ideal surprisal/code burden:

\[
C_M(N,P)=B_M+N[H(P)+D_{KL}(P\Vert Q_M)].
\]

Thus morphology `M_1` beats `M_2` iff

\[
D_{KL}(P\Vert Q_1)-D_{KL}(P\Vert Q_2)
<\frac{B_2-B_1}{N}.
\]

This yields a precise restricted answer:

> a morphology dominates when its inherited/developed bias matches the ecology enough to repay its development/build/lifecycle cost over the available horizon.

This is **information theory + amortization**, not ORION novelty.

### Why neural systems can dominate large regions

Existing parent theory/evidence gives plausible mechanisms:

- effective differentiable credit assignment;
- distributed/shared representations;
- very large training-to-inference amortization horizons;
- empirical scaling with data/compute;
- pretraining-induced transfer priors;
- exceptional tensor hardware/software co-design.

Their limitations are also ecology variables rather than universal defects:

- plasticity loss/interference;
- retraining/revision costs;
- exact verification requirements;
- regime shift;
- data/feedback scarcity;
- communication/memory costs.
