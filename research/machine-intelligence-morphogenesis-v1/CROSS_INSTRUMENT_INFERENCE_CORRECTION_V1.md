## What the registered measurements identify

[EFI-1–4](../gmi-grand-unification-v1/EMPIRICAL_FRONTIER_IDENTIFICATION_THEOREM_V1.md)
separates possible frontier membership, necessary membership and the set of
jointly feasible frontiers. An interval survivor is not proof of actual
coexistence. With XOR costs `(88,[1,3],[1,3])` and neural costs
`(312,[2,4],[2,4])`, the same evidence admits an XOR-only frontier and a
mixed frontier. These are different underlying worlds, not an observed
architecture transition. The exact integer/zero-width microscope example
retains its own instrument and candidate scope.

Likewise, matching family labels on a native Python meter and a virtual
machine is an agreement between two scoped results. Transport requires
matched task/adequacy, candidates, uncertainty support and dominance-preserving
resource mappings. Distinct tasks, omitted development costs or changed
instruments do not inherit such a theorem from the common label.

## A feasible falsifier

The fixed opcode register is `88,136,312,472`. XOR has the unique strict
minimum. Consequently it survives every timing realization under the
registered three-coordinate Pareto rule: no rival can dominate it in all
coordinates. A neural-only frontier is impossible under those apparatus
assumptions and cannot test a timing prediction.

The constructive replacement is to distinguish an XOR-only frontier from a
frontier also containing a neural candidate. Both patterns are feasible in
the finite controls. A new experiment must freeze its actual prediction,
candidate preparation, full cost accounting, joint uncertainty model and
feasible falsifier before its new data exist. This correction supplies the
identification method, not a fabricated prediction or measurement.
