# Information-theoretic bounded-rationality / phase-transition parent v1

Status: **major parent subtraction for morphology phase-law claims.**

## 1. Claim attacked

A weak Track-B field claim would be:

> Resource constraints and information-processing costs cause different organizations of intelligence to become optimal, including specialization, hierarchy and abstraction, with phase transitions as resource trade-offs change.

This broad statement is already strongly parent-owned.

## 2. Information-theoretic bounded rationality

Ortega, Braun and collaborators formalize bounded decision making with a free-energy objective of the form

\[
\max_p \; \mathbb E[U] - \frac{1}{\beta} D_{KL}(p\Vert p_0),
\]

trading utility against information-processing cost relative to a prior policy.

This framework:

- treats resource limits explicitly;
- connects processing resources to stochastic optimal policies;
- extends to sequential decision problems;
- supports hierarchical systems of bounded rational agents.

Gottwald & Braun (2019) specifically analyze systems of bounded-rational agents and show that limited information-processing capacity can make **specialization and hierarchical coordination** optimal organizational structures.

Therefore:

```text
RESOURCE_LIMITS_CAN_FAVOR_HIERARCHY_OR_SPECIALIZATION
```

is not a Track-B novelty claim.

## 3. Information Bottleneck phase transitions

Information Bottleneck optimizes a compression/prediction trade-off such as

\[
I(X;Z)-\beta I(Y;Z).
\]

Existing theory studies qualitative/phase changes of learned representations as `beta` changes, including sharp transitions between unlearnable and learnable regimes and the onset of new representational components/classes.

Therefore:

```text
RESOURCE/COMPRESSION_PARAMETER_CHANGE
-> REPRESENTATIONAL_PHASE_TRANSITION
```

is also parent-owned in an important class of models.

## 4. Consequence for the GMI phase programme

Track B receives no novelty credit for merely observing:

```text
small resource budget -> simple/generalized representation
large resource budget -> specialized/richer representation
```

or

```text
cost trade-off -> hierarchy / specialization emerges.
```

Strong parent terminal:

```text
INFORMATION_THEORETIC_BOUNDED_RATIONALITY_PARENT_SUFFICIENT_FOR_GENERIC_RESOURCE_PHASE_CLAIM
```

## 5. What remains open

The possible residual becomes considerably narrower:

> Can one predict **which concrete developmental-equivalence morphology family** (neural, probabilistic, programmatic, explicit epistemic, hybrid, etc.) will be Pareto-admissible from its actual structure/update law and heterogeneous physical/lifecycle resource vector, and can that prediction survive across paradigms and held-out ecologies?

Differences from the generic free-energy parent must include at least one of:

- deriving morphology-specific proposal/update geometry rather than assuming prior/action distributions;
- multiple heterogeneous resource coordinates not reducible to one chosen KL price without loss;
- persistent learning/maintenance/revision/plasticity over long lifetimes;
- morphology generation itself rather than choosing a policy within a fixed representation;
- exact verifier/authority constraints;
- prospective cross-paradigm prediction using one invariant family.

If none survives, the bounded-rationality parent is sufficient.

## 6. Parent-product danger

A particularly strong null now is:

```text
information-theoretic bounded rationality / rate-distortion
+ algorithm selection
+ meta-learning
+ AutoML / architecture search
```

This combination may already provide the normative selection theory plus the mechanism for finding a suitable architecture.

Track B must compare against that **product**, not each parent in isolation.

## 7. Implication for unknown morphology search

A phase-diagram hole cannot simply be:

> no current system sits at this utility/information-cost point.

A novel morphology candidate must exhibit a structural/developmental regime not already generated by the bounded-rationality + known-morphology parent product, and must remain non-equivalent under the registered resource/compiler relation.
