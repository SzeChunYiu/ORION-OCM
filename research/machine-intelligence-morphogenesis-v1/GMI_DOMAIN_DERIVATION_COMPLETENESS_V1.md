# GMI Domain-Derivation Completeness v1

Status: **FOUNDATIONAL THEORY HARDENING / PROSPECTIVE — NOT A CLAIM OF COMPLETE DOMAIN CLOSURE**

Status date: 2026-09-12.

Purpose:

> Make GMI capable of deriving, comparing and falsifying machine-intelligence domains rather than merely cataloguing architectures after they are invented.

The central requirement is stronger than representational universality. Any ordinary computable realization can be simulated by a universal machine; that fact alone collapses all domains into one vacuous software class. GMI therefore treats domains as **resource-sensitive native realization classes**.

---

# 1. Realization object

A machine realization is

\[
M=(Z,\mathcal A,K,U,\Gamma,\kappa,\rho),
\]

where:

```text
Z         cognitive/developmental state carrier
A         primitive native operator algebra on Z
K         execution law
U         within-form development/update law
Gamma     morphology-changing law
kappa     semantic interpretation/compiler relation
rho       lifecycle burden vector
```

A domain is not named by source-code syntax or physical packaging. It is characterized by the combination `(Z,A)` together with the native execution/development laws that exploit them.

---

# 2. Non-collapse principle

## GMI-DOM-1 — computability equivalence is insufficient

If two machine families can simulate one another only through a compiler whose overhead may grow without a registered bound in state size, precision, latency, memory, communication, search, update or verification burden, then computability equivalence does not establish domain equivalence.

Reason:

```text
Turing simulation preserves computability;
it does not preserve lifecycle burden, locality, precision, online adaptation, evidence semantics or morphology response.
```

Therefore a claim of domain reduction must include a semantics-preserving compiler with a declared burden transform.

---

# 3. Bounded reduction

For domains `D_a,D_b`, define a registered reduction on ecology family `E` as a compiler

\[
C_{a\to b}:M_a\mapsto M_b
\]

such that protected semantic traces are preserved to registered tolerance and

\[
\rho(M_b,e)\preceq T_{a\to b}(\rho(M_a,e),X_e)
\]

for every `e` in the registered family, where `T` is frozen before protected evaluation.

A **strong bidirectional domain equivalence** requires reductions both ways with accepted burden inflation.

A candidate new domain survives reduction only if every strongest known parent translation violates at least one registered condition:

```text
semantic preservation
bounded description size
bounded precision
bounded execution cost
bounded update/development cost
bounded communication/locality cost
bounded search cost
bounded verification/authority cost
bounded adaptation/morphogenesis cost
```

# 4. Domain-selection law

For obligation `O`, context/resource prices `P`, and developmental history `H`, define the best achievable burden in domain `D`:

\[
R_D(O,P,H)
=
\inf_{M\in D\cap Adm(O)}
\mathcal L_O(M)+\pi(P)^\top\rho(M;O,P,H).
\]

The predicted frontier domains are

\[
\mathcal D^*(O,P,H)
=
\arg\min_D R_D(O,P,H).
\]

This is the domain-level analogue of morphology selection.

The theory goal is not to assert one universally superior domain. It is to predict how `D*` changes under interventions on obligation structure, information geometry, developmental history and resource prices.

# 8. New theorem target: domain necessity by burden separation

## GMI-DOM-2 — burden-separation criterion

Let `C` be a candidate carrier/operator family and `B` the union of established parent domains. Suppose there exists an obligation sequence `{O_n}` and context sequence `{P_n}` such that:

1. a realization in `C` achieves protected adequacy with burden `r_C(n)`;
2. every semantics-preserving realization compiled into `B` has burden at least `r_B(n)`;
3. for at least one registered burden coordinate or monotone scalarization,

\[
\frac{r_B(n)}{r_C(n)}\to\infty
\]

or crosses a frozen material threshold over the protected range.

Then `C` is not reducible to `B` at the registered burden class.

This is not yet a universal theorem of ontology; it is the formal target for establishing a scientifically meaningful domain distinction.

# 9. Approximate-domain criterion

Real systems may not admit exact semantic equality. For tolerance `delta`, compare the efficient frontiers

\[
F_D(\delta)=\{\rho(M): M\in D,\; L_O(M)\le\delta\}.
\]

Domain separation is supported when a candidate exhibits a protected Pareto region not reachable by bounded reductions from established domains and the separation persists under:

```text
semantic remint
implementation remint
precision changes
substrate repricing
multiple search encodings
parent retuning
```

Approximate frontier separation is evidence, not a proof of absolute ontological distinctness.

# 10. Domain completeness levels

Use:

```text
DC0 CATALOGUE
    major known computing paradigms have typed homes.

DC1 REDUCTION MAP
    pairwise strongest-parent reductions/compilers are registered.

DC2 NECESSITY MAP
    each retained domain has at least one obligation family with a theorem/bound or protected burden separation.

DC3 PREDICTIVE DOMAIN LAW
    pre-outcome ecology descriptors predict domain frontier occupancy on held families.

DC4 NEUTRAL DOMAIN RECOVERY
    low-level carrier/operator search rediscovers known domains without domain macros.

DC5 OPEN-DOMAIN DISCOVERY
    neutral search repeatedly recovers a carrier/operator complex outside the frozen basis, followed by parent reduction failure and fresh replication.

DC6 RECURSIVE DOMAIN CLOSURE
    every discovered residual is reduced, incorporated as a new domain/kingdom, falsified, or explicitly open with a closure experiment.
```

Current status: **DC0 strong, DC1 partial, DC2 partial, DC3-DC6 open**.

# 11. GMI must explain known domains, not merely contain them

For every baseline domain the theory must derive why its native carrier becomes favorable.

Examples:

### coefficient/function-field
Favored when broad regularity can be compressed into reusable parameterized maps and amortized over many queries.

### exemplar/memory-indexed
Favored when individual distinctions are numerous/volatile and local insertion or retrieval is cheaper than global recompression.

### probabilistic-belief
Favored when protected decisions depend materially on calibrated uncertainty, latent alternatives or evidence integration.

### symbolic/program
Favored when compositional exactness, discrete rules, verification or algorithmic reuse dominates approximation burden.

### search/frontier
Favored when the answer cannot be cheaply compiled ahead of time and sparse online expansion/evaluation beats exhaustive representation.

### dynamical-state
Favored when sufficient cognition is inherently history-dependent and a compact evolving state is cheaper than replaying full history.

### collective/distributed
Favored when information/resources are physically or legally partitioned and the target quotient is reconstructible only through communication/coordination.

### morphogenetic/self-rewriting
Favored when the ecology changes enough that fixed realization family regret exceeds morphology-switch/search cost.

These statements are programmes until quantified and protected.

## N8 — Constructive / Autocatalytic Intelligence

Primitive state:

```text
population of constructors that can synthesize, modify or replicate other computational components
```

Native operation:

```text

```

The sufficient developmental state is the reachable constructor closure, not merely current outputs or one program.

Parent attacks:

```text
morphogenetic systems
program synthesis
chemical reaction networks
population/evolutionary systems
```

Support requires showing that constructive closure predicts future cognition with a burden law not reducible to ordinary self-rewriting or search.
