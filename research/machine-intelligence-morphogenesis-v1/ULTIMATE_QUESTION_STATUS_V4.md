# Ultimate question

> Is there a general theory of machine intelligence with a fundamental cognitive substrate from which neural, symbolic, probabilistic, programmatic, OCM-like and possibly unknown intelligence forms arise; can it explain their developmental limits and predict new forms?

# Current answer in one paragraph

At the **computational, representation-independent level**, current evidence does not support one unique irreducible cognitive atom. Unit boundaries can be split/refactored or merged/flattened, universal formalisms encode the same computations, and meaningful minimality appears only after a future-obligation/equivalence/resource contract is fixed. Known machine-intelligence architectures are more usefully treated as **adaptive factorizations / inductive biases / developmental geometries**. Which one dominates is conditional on ecology, information, verification, hardware and lifetime cost. The broad scaffold is largely reconstructible from mature parent theory: universal computation/search, state abstraction, graphical/factored models, Bayesian/statistical learning, optimization, algorithm selection, resource rationality, AutoML/evolution, meta-learning and modularity/evolvability. The strongest still-open Track-B residual is therefore not a new tuple or universal computer, but a **prospective cross-paradigm law connecting measurable machine/ecology structure to developmental burden and endogenous morphology transitions**, ideally predicting a parent-frontier hole before discovering a new form.

# Q1 — What is the fundamental cognitive unit?

## Strong answer we can now give

A **universal representation-independent unit boundary is not identifiable from computation alone**.

Reasons already formalized in Track B:

1. a unit can often be replaced by a composition of smaller units;
2. a finite network of units can be flattened into one global finite-state transducer;
3. universal program languages compile between many candidate bases;
4. description length under universal languages is invariant only up to compiler constants;
5. minimal generating bases need not be syntactically unique;
6. finite measured burden/behavior does not identify a unique internal morphology.

So the naive statement

> one unique smallest atom exists independently of representation, task, resources and physics

is not supported.

## What *is* mathematically meaningful

Fix a cognitive obligation and all future interventions/observations that matter. Then quotient states/configurations that are indistinguishable under every such future sequence.

For a finite deterministic developmental machine this produces a minimal future-trace state representation, parented by automata/transducer minimization. Track B's exact fixture shows:

```text
current-query quotient       {0,1}, {2,3}
developmental quotient       {0}, {1}, {2,3}
```

States 0 and 1 look identical now but respond differently to future teaching; therefore development changes what distinctions are minimally necessary.

The correct concept is:

> **minimal sufficient developmental state relative to a registered future cognitive obligation.**

Not a metaphysical cognitive atom.

For approximate/stochastic systems, bisimulation/state-abstraction/predictive-state parents receive first refusal.

## Why local units still matter

Even when the exact global developmental quotient is enormous, a good factorization can be compact.

The exact independent-unit calibration gives:

\[
|S_{min}|=3^n,
\qquad
|T_{flat}|=2n3^n,
\]

while the factorized realization needs `n` local state cells and one six-entry shared local rule, and a teaching event changes one cell.

This explains the engineering/scientific importance of factorization without making the factors universal atoms.

Parent verdict: factored MDPs/DBNs/programs already own the generic compactness principle.

# Q3 — What limits a form of intelligence, and why does one form dominate?

## Parent-level general answer

Different morphologies encode different inductive/search/update biases and different resource tradeoffs.

A useful schematic is:

\[
\text{lifetime burden}
=
\text{build/development}
+
\text{ecology--bias mismatch}
+
\text{execution/update/verification/maintenance}.
\]

In an idealized proposal model this becomes exactly:

\[
C_M=B_M+N[H(P)+D_{KL}(P\Vert Q_M)].
\]

For costed Markov development, stochastic-shortest-path / metareasoning parents apply. Given a suitable progress potential, drift theory converts expected local progress into first-hitting bounds. The cross-paradigm toy calibration shows program search, Bayesian updating and quadratic gradient descent all admit a `potential / drift -> hitting time` description—but this is drift-analysis parent territory.

## Factorization adds a second major axis

Ecology dependency structure can select different factorizations under build/communication/horizon prices. In the exact four-variable partition census:

```text
structured pair ecology + short/medium horizon -> matching pair modules
uniform + very short horizon                   -> singleton/no-build factors
long horizon                                   -> monolithic factorization when cross-module cost dominates
```

The pairwise phase relation is exact, but graph partitioning/modularity/factored-model parents own the generic principle.

## Therefore neural dominance is not mysterious in one sentence

Neural morphologies occupy a large practical region because they combine some mixture of:

```text
powerful distributed representation
effective differentiable credit assignment
large-scale amortization from training into cheap repeated inference
shared representation / statistical generalization
massive hardware/software co-design
```

while other regimes can favor explicit/programmatic/probabilistic/hybrid organizations because of exactness, local revision, sparse compositionality, uncertainty, verification, or different resource horizons.

No universal winner should exist without restricting the ecology/resource class.

# Ultimate answer at the present evidence level

> **There is currently no evidence for one representation-independent irreducible “cognitive atom” from which all machine intelligence uniquely follows. A more defensible general view is that machine-intelligence forms are alternative resource-bounded adaptive factorizations/inductive biases. Experience and ecology shape which factorizations are reachable and economical; known theory already explains many such effects, including architecture search, modularity emergence, resource-conditioned specialization and learning-to-learn. ORION-OCM has not yet discovered a new general theory of machine intelligence. Its strongest foundational opportunity is to discover a prospective cross-paradigm law linking structure and verified developmental history to future developmental geometry, and its strongest empirical opportunity is to show the same governed developmental effect across real language/math/code/science regimes.**

This is an upward answer because known lower-level mechanisms are absorbed rather than renamed. It is also falsifiable: if R1/R2 fail against parent products, Track B should become a metrology/theory-synthesis programme rather than a claim to a new fundamental law.
