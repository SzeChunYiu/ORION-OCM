## A native lower bound is not an exact cost

Condition on written-XOR cost X_n=3n+2 and delegating Python cost6 for n>=1.
Supply native cost N_n in a common additive resource unit; opcode accounting
alone does not establish physical commensurability. If N_n>=2n+1 only,
then D_n>=2n+7.

- Below5, X_n<2n+7 certifies XOR cheaper.
- At5, native11 gives a tie while native12 makes XOR cheaper.
- At6, native13/14/15 all meet the lower bound and make delegation
  cheaper/tied/dearer than XOR.
- An additional **exact** premise N_n=2n+1 gives X_n−D_n=n−5.

Positive scaling by sweep size2^n preserves these comparisons.
The table(88,104),(224,240),(544,544),(1280,1216) at n3–6 is formula
evaluation under equality, not a new native experiment.
The stated n loads+(n−1) additions+one return equals2n, not2n+1.
A hypothetical exact charge2n shifts the tie to4; charge4n keeps XOR cheaper
for every n>=1. “Faithful” alone therefore does not fix a crossover.
No claim about all neural architectures follows from a written template.
Existing PN execution reports retain their independent source, runtime,
measurement and cost-contract evidence obligations.

## Positive construction and remaining inference

A complete finite candidate/schedule register with exact adequacy and joint
costs supports constructive Pareto filtering and family identification.
Missing information is harmless when every compatible completion preserves
the answer. Equal-profile fibers or changing optimal candidates can prevent
a unique label or common construction even after profile closure.

Acquired-method learning or transfer additionally requires a demonstrated
acquisition, an identified executable state and consumer, and the appropriate
controlled comparison under declared evaluation and full costs. A memory
routing witness or local gradient control does not establish that conclusion.
The current patch performs no additional campaign or capability evaluation.
