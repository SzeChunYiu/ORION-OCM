## One line

**The controller programme is closed at this grammar (2026-09-12).** An integrated developmental controller
(library by validation → probe with history-learned depth → task-statement rule → liveness) costs half of an
*interleaving* parent holding the same library. That halving is the guided-first serving identity, not
controller evidence. Against the absorbed guided-first parent (one fixed choice: MDL library, depth 4), every
learned component is neutral or negative on 8/8 independently authored worlds. What history does buy is real
and stands: the mined library, served guided-first, beats RESET by 52–95 % on every authored world, and the
benefit replicates on novel arrangements (d = 2) on 9/9 seeds. Every claim here was earned or retracted by a
recorded test under one invariant: *never read the solution.*

**Why the revision loop is over, in four results.**
- *Serving identity*: per target, 0.500–0.501× the interleaving parent (INTEGRATED_ARM.md).
- *Zero residual*: 0/8 worlds where the controller is > 5 % below the guided-first parent; on four it reduces to
  that parent exactly (#411).
- *Selection unavailable from the tuning stream*: the validation stream robustly ranks libraries the wrong way on
  hc08 and hc09, with bootstrap P(pick the scored best) = 0.018 and 0.001 (ledger row 56).
- *Remaining headroom is at an unmineable ceiling*: only the author's true chunks beat the mined libraries, and
  those libraries themselves beat the true chunks on three worlds (#412, ledger row 58).
The mixed-regime continual line closed separately, with three stop rules fired and C3 structural (#407).
