## 2. External parents, with citations

Particle / population inference — approximating an answer by the consensus over
a population of many weak samples — is not novel here and is not claimed to be.
Neither is the descriptor-closure construction, the majority-vote aggregation,
or the charged-cost model.

- N. Metropolis and S. Ulam (1949), "The Monte Carlo method", *Journal of the
  American Statistical Association* 44(247):335–341. DOI `10.1080/01621459.1949.10483310`;
  and N. Metropolis, A. W. Rosenbluth, M. N. Rosenbluth, A. H. Teller and
  E. Teller (1953), "Equation of state calculations by fast computing machines",
  *The Journal of Chemical Physics* 21(6):1087–1092. DOI `10.1063/1.1699114`.
  The sample population as the computational object; the answer is read off the
  population, not off any one sample.
- N. J. Gordon, D. J. Salmond and A. F. M. Smith (1993), "Novel approach to
  nonlinear/non-Gaussian Bayesian state estimation", *IEE Proceedings F*
  140(2):107–113. DOI `10.1049/ip-f-2.1993.0015`; and G. Kitagawa (1996),
  "Monte Carlo filter and smoother for non-Gaussian nonlinear state space
  models", *Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics* 5(1):1–25.
  DOI `10.1080/10618600.1996.10474692`. The bootstrap/auxiliary particle filter:
  a population of weighted particles whose aggregate is the estimate; one
  particle is a sample, the population is the answer.
- A. Doucet, N. de Freitas and N. Gordon (eds., 2001), *Sequential Monte Carlo
  Methods in Practice*, Springer. DOI `10.1007/978-1-4757-3437-9`; and
  A. Doucet, S. Godsill and C. Andrieu (2000), "On sequential Monte Carlo
  sampling methods for Bayesian filtering", *Statistics and Computing*
  10(3):197–208. DOI `10.1023/A:1008935410038`. The particle-population
  approximation of a distribution, and the distinction between a marginal read
  and the joint population.
- P. Del Moral (2004), *Feynman-Kac Formulae: Genealogical and Interacting
  Particle Systems with Applications*, Springer. DOI `10.1007/978-1-4684-9393-1`.
  The mean-field / normalised-consensus analysis of an interacting particle
  population — the theoretical reason a population's consensus carries
  information a single particle does not.
- M. Rosenblatt (1956), "Remarks on some nonparametric estimates of a density
  function", *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 27(3):832–837.
  DOI `10.1214/aoms/1177728190`; and E. Parzen (1962), "On estimation of a
  probability density function and mode", *The Annals of Mathematical
  Statistics* 33(3):1065–1076. DOI `10.1214/aoms/1177704472`. The local
  averaging over a sample population: the estimate is an aggregate over
  neighbouring samples.
- L. Breiman (1996), "Bagging predictors", *Machine Learning* 24(2):123–140.
  DOI `10.1007/BF00058655`; and T. G. Dietterich (2000), "Ensemble methods in
  machine learning", in *Multiple Classifier Systems*, LNCS 1857, Springer,
  1–15. DOI `10.1007/3-540-45014-9_1`. The consensus over many weak members:
  the aggregate beats its members, and no single member is the mechanism.
- D. E. Knuth (1997), *The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 2*, 3rd ed.,
  Addison-Wesley. The multiplicative-hash permutation used as the registered
  presentation lever.
- G. Salton, A. Wong and C. S. Yang (1975), "A vector space model for automatic
  indexing", *Communications of the ACM* 18(11):613–620.
  DOI `10.1145/361219.361220`. Descriptor-term indexing over a document
  vocabulary; the descriptor-closure vocabulary is the same construction on a
  word list.

## 3. What is NOT claimed novel

- No particle-filter, sequential-Monte-Carlo, ensemble or density-estimation
  method here is new.
- "A short descriptor that recurs across the vocabulary is a strong feature" is
  a property of the corpus, not a discovery.
- The readout language, the recovery procedure, the post-hoc classifier, the
  charged-cost model and the eleven-coordinate ledger are the parents' forms,
  re-implemented here rather than imported.

## 4. The residual contribution of this tranche

One thing, methodological and small: **the family-blind recovery of the
population-plurality readout `PLUR` (the mode over a stored population of weak
particle votes) as the winner of a registered recovery over a closed,
pre-outcome readout language that includes the siblings' membership,
association, threshold-conjunction and neighbourhood-vote reads, at real scale,
with a matched single-particle-store negative control and a matched
particle-reweight design null** — and the demonstration that the recovered class
(`POPULATION_PLURALITY`) is distinct from the siblings'
(`STORED_EXEMPLAR_MEMBERSHIP`, `CUE_ASSOCIATION_FANOUT`,
`THRESHOLD_CONJUNCTION`) on the same source: the answer is the consensus over a
population, which none of the siblings' winners reads. A single particle already
agrees with that consensus 84.25% of the time, and still loses by a factor of
19.7 — which is exactly the property the population carries and the item does
not. The neighbouring Section-H rows are therefore separated by their readouts,
not by relabeling.
