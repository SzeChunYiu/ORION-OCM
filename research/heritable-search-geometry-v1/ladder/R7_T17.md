# A_T17 @ R7 — the tamper channel across system boundaries

**Verdict: LIFT_CONDITIONAL** (conditions named below are exactly the exchange
topologies that close the channel).
**PARENT_STATEMENT_UNVERIFIED_TEXT**: Baez–Fong 2015 open Markov processes —
recalled form used here: a Markov process with designated boundary vertices
where probability flux enters/leaves; the open master equation splits the
generator into interior plus boundary-coupling terms, and stationary states
satisfy flux balance. Exact theorem statements not verified against source.

## Setting

T17 toy (given): a verifier receiving external evidence admits tampering —
evidence crossing a boundary can be chosen by the outside for effect, not
truth. R7 formalizes: family F with internal evaluator node v; boundary
vertices B; exchange = flux through B. The tamper channel is the coupling term
in the generator that lets boundary state influence v's transition rates.

## What closes the channel (the surviving statement)

Write the open generator as H = H_int + H_coup where H_coup carries the
dependence on boundary state. The tamper channel is OPEN at v iff
∂H_coup/∂(boundary state) ≠ 0 on any path reaching v. Therefore:

1. **CLOSED: decoupled-evaluator topology.** v's connected component has zero
   H_coup — no boundary term touches any transition of v. Evidence may flow
   elsewhere in the family; v's law is autonomous. (Trivial but honest: this
   is the closed-evaluator corner; it closes the channel by renouncing the
   exchange at v.)
2. **CLOSED: pre-committed diode.** Boundary flux may reach v only through a
   fixed channel Q frozen into the constitution BEFORE any evidence arrived,
   with Q provenance-stripping: Q depends on content only, and Q itself is
   independent of boundary state (a constant channel in the generator). The
   composite boundary→v then factors through a constant map, so no adversary
   selection at B changes v's transition law. This is the nontrivial closure:
   exchange survives, tampering does not — because the interface is not
   selectable after seeing traffic.
3. **OPEN: any two-way exchange at v** (v's state readable outside): the
   reflection alone is a channel — an outside selector conditioning its
   evidence on observed v-state realizes a selection channel (cf. T08 leak
   mode), even if the inbound side is a diode.
4. **OPEN: any post-hoc or performance-indexed interface** — selecting Q by
   past performance makes Q a G_t-measurable-but-side-fed function; this is
   exactly the T08 SEL-MEAS violation.

## Condition (named)

DIODE-PRECOMMIT: exchange topologies close the tamper channel iff every
boundary→evaluator path factors through a constant channel committed before
the exchange opens, and no evaluator state crosses outward. Both clauses are
checkable as wiring facts (generator-level decoupling), not semantic facts —
which is why they are governable at R7 despite T15.

## Note

The analogy to balanced-flux stationarity (open Markov boundary flux balance)
supplies the vocabulary, not the theorem: flux balance constrains *amounts*,
while the tamper channel is about *coupling structure*; the closure conditions
above are strictly about the latter. This gap is why the verdict is
conditional rather than a parent lift.
