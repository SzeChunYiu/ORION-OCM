## 1. Purpose

The Grand-GMI theory now contains separate laws for semantic quotients, causal cuts, local transformation complexity, symmetry, distributed composition, realization compilation, physical-resource transport, family selection, architecture refinement, empirical resource uncertainty, developmental reachability, generalization, continuous approximation, exact non-neural compilation, and active closed-loop intelligence.

This theorem defines that certificate and the boundary beyond which more theorem prose cannot substitute for missing physical or empirical facts.

## 2. Registered derivation certificate

For a declared scope `Sigma`, an NN/non-NN derivation certificate contains the following load-bearing fields.

### C1 — problem registration

A typed GMI problem

\[
\mathcal G=(\mathbf P,\mathcal E,\Omega,\rho,Q)
\]

with protected observables/interventions, candidate boundary and selection rule.

### C2 — semantic state evidence

A protected response equivalence/quotient `S*`, or a certified finite-resolution/approximate semantic representation when exact quotienting is not the registered target.

### C3 — cut and transformation requirements

The registered semantic-cut requirements `kappa` and local transformation requirements `tau`, including any exact lower bounds, approximation tolerances or unresolved infima.

### C4 — operational architecture constraints

Derived properties such as locality, equivariance, persistent state, dynamic routing, graph aggregation, sparse conditional routing, active sensing, closed-loop feedback or other protected architecture-signature constraints.

### C5 — realization witnesses

For every candidate morphology used in selection, a realization witness showing that the candidate actually implements or approximates the protected operational process at the declared tolerance. Neural, non-neural, hybrid, quantum, analog, biological and other families are admitted symmetrically.

### C6 — physical resource evidence

A proved or measured substrate resource profile, lower/upper bound or uncertainty set with declared calibration/coverage semantics. Synthetic point costs are valid only for synthetic theorem witnesses, not as claims about real hardware.

### C7 — developmental reachability

An admitted initial condition, update/search/training/evolution law and development budget, together with a proof, enumeration, bound or empirical certificate that each selected candidate is reachable.

### C8 — deployment/generalization evidence

Evidence that capability holds on the protected deployment ecology: exact enumeration, structural transport theorem, statistical certificate, verified invariant or another registered justification. Training-set fit alone is insufficient when the obligation concerns unseen ecology.

### C9 — provenance and consistency

Executable receipts, theorem ledger and frozen claims must agree. If a checker regenerated from the committed source contradicts its receipt or prose aggregate, the affected certificate is invalid until reconciled.

### C10 — candidate-set scope

The derivation must state the realization/candidate universe over which selection was performed. A unique result inside a registered set is not automatically a theorem about every physically possible intelligence morphology.

## 3. DC-1 — certificate sufficiency theorem

Consider a finite registered candidate set `M` for which C1–C10 are decidable at the declared scope. For each `m in M`, determine:

1. realization validity;
2. obligation adequacy on the protected deployment ecology;
3. substrate legality;
4. developmental reachability;
5. hard-budget feasibility;
6. registered resource/capability profile or certified uncertainty set.

Remove candidates failing any hard condition. Apply the declared robust Pareto/selection rule to the survivors, obtaining selected set `M*`.

Then the selected family support

\[
F^*=\{family(m):m\in M^*\}
\]

is exactly decidable for that finite registered certificate.

This theorem is deliberately scope-indexed. It is not a total optimizer for unrestricted Turing-complete, infinite-dimensional or noncompact morphology spaces.

## 4. DC-2 — canonical family verdicts

For a valid certificate:

- `M* = empty` -> **INFEASIBLE_AT_REGISTERED_SCOPE**;
- `F* = {NEURAL}` -> **DERIVED_NEURAL**;
- `F* = {NON_NEURAL}` -> **DERIVED_NON_NEURAL**;
- `F* = {HYBRID}` -> **DERIVED_HYBRID**;
- `|F*| > 1` -> **FAMILY_COEXISTENCE / NOT_UNIQUE**.

If a load-bearing certificate field is absent, invalid or genuinely underidentified so that compatible evidence worlds give different verdicts, the result is

**UNDECIDED_FROM_CURRENT_EVIDENCE**.

`UNDECIDED` is therefore a formal result, not a failure to choose a fashionable architecture.

## 5. DC-3 — named-architecture verdict

A family verdict is coarser than a named architecture verdict.

A name such as CNN, Transformer, RNN, GNN or neural MoE is derived only if:

1. the name has a registered operational definition;
2. every selected morphology satisfies that definition, or a declared unique rule selects its equivalence class;
3. operationally distinct competitors are excluded by obligation, resource, reachability or other certified evidence.

Thus Grand GMI can derive `LOCAL_TRANSLATION_EQUIVARIANT`, `PERSISTENT_STATE_REQUIRED`, `DYNAMIC_ROUTING_REQUIRED`, `PERMUTATION_EQUIVARIANT_AGGREGATION` or `SPARSE_EXPERT_ROUTING` without falsely claiming a unique historical software architecture.

## 6. DC-4 — candidate-universe sensitivity theorem

Let `M subset M'`. A family verdict that is unique on `M` need not remain unique on `M'` because a newly admitted adequate morphology can dominate or coexist with the previous selection.

Therefore every derivation claim is explicitly relative to the registered candidate universe unless a separate completeness theorem proves that all relevant physical realizations have been covered.

This is the correct open-world boundary for claims such as "GMI derives a neural network." The rigorous form is:

> Under registered problem `Sigma`, candidate universe `M`, substrate/resource evidence, development law and deployment certificate, the selected reachable frontier has family support `{NEURAL}`.

Anything stronger requires stronger coverage evidence.

`CANDIDATE_UNIVERSE_COVERAGE_CORRECTION_V1.md` supplies the missing
completeness condition in checkable form. Because a family is the extension of
a structural predicate rather than a list, coverage is validity of the
disjunction of the registered predicates (CU-1), and a verdict over a proved
cover whose every class carries a derived lower bound does extend to machines
nobody has built (CU-2). Two boundaries come with it: adjoining the complement
predicate always completes a cover, so the binding question is whether every
class in it is *bounded*, and completing a cover frequently withdraws a verdict
rather than confirming it; and without a coverage proof the covered-class
verdict carries no information at all about the residue (CU-3b), so it must be
reported as `ROBUST_WITHIN_COVERED_CLASSES_WITH_OPEN_RESIDUE`. Coverage must
also be discharged at the resolution at which the verdict is stated, since a
cover of components does not lift to composites (CU-4).

## 7. DC-5 — evidence-world robustness theorem

Let `W` be the set of worlds compatible with all registered uncertainty sets and statistical/physical evidence. A unique family verdict is evidence-robust only if the same family support is selected in every `w in W` on the declared confidence event.

If two compatible evidence worlds produce different family supports, the certificate verdict is `UNDECIDED_FROM_CURRENT_EVIDENCE`.

This composes the Empirical Resource Identification and Generalization/Ecology layers into one rule: uncertain resource and uncertain deployment capability are not allowed to disappear when the final architecture label is printed.

## 9. DC-7 — irreducible input theorem

Grand GMI cannot derive the following from semantic structure alone:

1. **arbitrary obligation/value** — physical dynamics do not choose a unique nontrivial goal or constitution;
2. **actual substrate resource laws/numbers** — energy, latency, memory, noise, precision and material costs require physical law and measurement/model evidence;
3. **actual deployment ecology** — unseen environment distributions or adversarial conditions require assumptions, data, invariants or interventions;
4. **actual development law/reachability** — endpoint existence does not imply SGD, search, evolution or synthesis reaches it within budget;
5. **candidate-universe completeness** — absence of an unregistered competitor is not proved by failing to imagine one;
6. **parent-theory hypotheses** — universal approximation, control separation, quantum-channel laws, thermodynamic inequalities, statistical-learning bounds and similar imported results apply only when their own assumptions hold.

These are not missing algebraic steps inside Grand GMI. They are external physical/empirical premises that any theory of realized intelligence must obtain from the world.

## 10. DC-8 — formal closure criterion

For the question

> "How can Grand GMI derive neural-network and non-neural intelligence?"

the formal derivation framework is closed at a registered scope when C1–C10 are either:

- supplied and validated, producing a family/property verdict; or
- explicitly marked unresolved, producing `UNDECIDED`/`INFEASIBLE` with the missing premise named.

A further theorem round is required only if there exists a logically necessary bridge from supplied premises to the requested verdict that is neither covered by an existing Grand-GMI theorem nor delegated to a clearly identified parent theory.

Missing real measurements, missing environment data or an undecidable unrestricted search problem do **not** become formal gaps merely because the desired architecture label has not yet been selected.

## 12. What Grand GMI now explains about neural intelligence

A neural realization can be **derived** when the obligation first forces operational structure—such as local equivariance, persistent memory, dynamic routing, graph aggregation, continuous approximation or active feedback—and a neural compiler realizes that structure, after which measured/proved resources, reachable training dynamics and deployment evidence leave only neural realizations on the selected frontier.

Universal approximation contributes realizability, not necessity. Training dynamics contribute reachability, not semantic definition. Hardware contributes cost, not value. The final neural verdict is the intersection of all of them.

## 13. What Grand GMI now explains about non-neural intelligence

A non-neural realization can be **derived** directly from finite semantic structure—e.g. a minimal exact finite-state transducer, truth table, Boolean circuit or straight-line program—or from another substrate-specific parent realization theorem. It is selected when those realizations satisfy the obligation and dominate/exclude neural competitors under the registered physical, developmental and deployment evidence.

Non-neural systems may still be recurrent, adaptive, distributed, actively sensing, self-modifying or compositional. Those are operational properties, not synonyms for neurality.
