# Rice parent subtraction v1 — `gmi-833-ab-residual-definitions-v1` (issue #833, AB08)

Row AB08, verbatim from comment 5684607872:

    - [ ] Explicitly parent-subtract Rice-style **algorithm selection**: problem space, feature space, algorithm space, performance space, selection mapping.

**Parent.** Rice, J. R., "The algorithm selection problem", *Advances in
Computers* 15 (1976), pp. 65–118, doi:10.1016/S0065-2458(08)60520-3.
Modern restatement: Smith-Miles, K., "Cross-disciplinary perspectives on
meta-learning for algorithm selection", *ACM Computing Surveys* 41(1), 2008,
doi:10.1145/1456650.1456656. Both `CITE-TF`: canonical field anchors entered
from field knowledge, **not** re-verified against a live source in this
tranche. AC05 is not earned here.

Rice's model is a five-tuple: a **problem space** `P`, a **feature space** `F`
with an extraction map `f : P → F`, an **algorithm space** `A`, a
**performance space** `R^n` with a performance map `p : A × P → R^n`, and a
**selection mapping** `S : F → A` chosen to maximize `‖p(S(f(x)), x)‖`. The
crosswalk cites Rice at row 25 (`selection`); it does not subtract him. This
file does.

**The subtraction discipline.** For each of the five components: what Rice's
object is, what plays that role in GMI, a verdict in the closed vocabulary
`ABSORBED` / `PARTIAL` / `DIVERGENT`, what Rice already supplies, and the named
residual. `ABSORBED` means Rice owns the component outright and GMI claims
nothing there.

---

## Component 1 — problem space

**Rice's object.** A set `P` of problem instances, given extensionally, over
which performance is measured.

**GMI's object.** The *ecology* / task distribution: a set of instances plus a
sampling law over them.

**Verdict.** `ABSORBED`.

**What Rice already supplies.** The whole construction. Rice's `P` is an
arbitrary instance set and imposes no structure GMI's ecology violates; a
sampling law over `P` is the standard measure-theoretic completion and is not a
new object.

**Residual.** None. GMI claims nothing here, and any GMI statement of the form
"the ecology is a novel construct" is refuted by this row.

---

## Component 2 — feature space

**Rice's object.** A space `F` with an extraction map `f : P → F`. Rice states
plainly that the choice of features is the hard part and that the model is
parameterized by it.

**GMI's object.** Ecology descriptors: the measured properties of a task
distribution that a selection rule is allowed to read.

**Verdict.** `ABSORBED`.

**What Rice already supplies.** `f : P → F` and the observation that selection
quality is bounded by feature adequacy. A descriptor vector over a task
distribution is `f` applied to a distribution rather than an instance, which is
Rice's model over the space of distributions — a change of index set, not of
structure.

**Residual.** None. The residual that *looks* like one — "descriptors of a
distribution rather than of an instance" — is a re-indexing Rice's formalism
already admits.

---

## Component 3 — algorithm space

**Rice's object.** A set `A` of algorithms, **given in advance**, over which
`S` ranges. Rice's `A` is a portfolio: finite, enumerated, and labelled.

**GMI's object.** A generated candidate space — programs enumerated from a
grammar, not a curated portfolio — whose members are not labelled in advance
and may not have been seen before.

**Verdict.** `PARTIAL`.

**What Rice already supplies.** The role of `A` in the model, the requirement
that `S` range over it, and the fact that `S` can do no better than the best
member of `A` on any instance. Every GMI statement of the form "selection cannot
beat the best available candidate" is Rice's, not GMI's.

**Residual.** *(named)* Rice's model says nothing about where `A` comes from or
what happens when `A` is generated by a grammar rather than curated. The
residual is the **grammar-induced structure of `A`**: that the enumeration
order, the reachable subset, and the density of the candidate space are
determined by the grammar, and therefore that a selection result is confounded
with a generator bias unless the generator is held fixed or varied deliberately.
This residual is **owned by `gmi-833-g0-grammar-bias-v1`**, not invented here,
and it is what makes AA30 and AA31 (search- and grammar-induced artifacts)
distinct open rows rather than instances of Rice.

---

## Component 4 — performance space

**Rice's object.** `R^n` with `p : A × P → R^n`, and a norm `‖·‖` collapsing the
vector to a scalar. Rice is explicit that the norm is a modelling choice.

**GMI's object.** A resource-indexed performance vector — accuracy together with
compute, memory and sample cost — compared by Pareto dominance rather than by a
scalarization.

**Verdict.** `PARTIAL`.

**What Rice already supplies.** The vector-valued performance map, the
observation that `n > 1` is the normal case, and the warning that the choice of
norm determines the answer. GMI's "arbitrary scalarization dependence" concern
(AA29) is Rice's warning restated.

**Residual.** *(named)* Rice collapses to a scalar via a norm and proceeds;
refusing to collapse, and reporting the Pareto set itself as the selection
output, is a different operation with different theory (dominance is a partial
order, so "the best algorithm" may not exist). That refusal is **owned by
`gmi-833-finite-pareto-density-v1`**. The residual claimable here is only the
*statement* that the refusal is a departure from Rice, not any result about it.

---

## Component 5 — selection mapping

**Rice's object.** `S : F → A`, chosen to maximize performance. Rice studies the
existence, approximation and learning of `S` from data.

**GMI's object.** A selection law predicting a well-performing candidate from
ecology descriptors, including for candidate families not present in the
training portfolio.

**Verdict.** `PARTIAL`.

**What Rice already supplies.** The map, its type, the criterion, the learning
formulation, and the negative results that bound it — with Wolpert & Macready,
"No free lunch theorems for optimization", *IEEE TEC* 1(1), 1997,
doi:10.1109/4235.585893, supplying the impossibility of a uniformly best `S`.
Any GMI claim that "selection must be ecology-relative" is Rice plus NFL.

**Residual.** *(named)* Rice's `S` has codomain `A`, a set fixed before `S` is
learned. A map whose codomain includes candidates **absent from the fitting
portfolio** is not an instance of Rice's `S`: it is an extrapolation problem,
and it needs its own identifiability argument saying what makes an unseen
candidate's performance predictable at all. That argument is **not supplied
here and is not supplied anywhere on `main`**; naming it is the residual. It is
the honest version of the "unseen form" claim and it is a *gap*, not a result.

---

## What is NOT claimed novel with respect to Rice

- The four-space decomposition itself, in any form or renaming.
- Any statement that selection is relative to a problem distribution.
- Any statement that features bound selection quality.
- Any statement that performance is vector-valued and that scalarization is a
  choice.
- Any statement that no selector is uniformly best (that is Wolpert & Macready).
- Any statement that a selector cannot beat the best member of its own
  portfolio.

Of the five components, **two are `ABSORBED` and three are `PARTIAL`**; **zero**
are `DIVERGENT`. Every named residual is either owned by another package on
`main` or is explicitly a gap with no result behind it. **This file does not
establish that GMI is novel with respect to Rice**; it establishes what would
have to be true for such a claim to be assertable, and records that three of the
five places it could live are named and two of them are already owned elsewhere.
