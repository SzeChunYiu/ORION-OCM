The finite query-tree model now charges the chosen adequate action as well as
the queries. A table of attainable terminal cost vectors on every labelled
subcube reconstructs all no-repeat profiles; its pointwise antichains
reconstruct the exact frontier of all finite deterministic trees.

The sharp repair is the missing terminal charge. A task can admit immediate
stopping at cost 3 while one query enables total cost 1. The earlier RQR
theorem remains correct for its explicitly restricted query-cost coordinate.

No randomized controller, learned semantics, implementation storage or full
physical/lifetime-cost theorem follows. The general vector-backup mechanism
is established prior work, specialized here to this precise GMI interface.
