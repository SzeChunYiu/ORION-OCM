## C1 — the state-class choice law and its real-system extension

### OBJ-1 (material) — the registered null cannot catch a boundary misplaced by less than 50%, and the quantity that *could* catch every misplacement was not used

`gmi-833-z-z15-decisive-falsifiers-v1/FREEZE_V1.md`, §"Null the true result must beat": *"200 randomized boundary laws `lambda*_rand = r·eta·p/2` with `r` drawn from a seeded rational ladder excluding `r = 1`. Each must be **caught** by `F1`. Target: `200/200` random boundaries falsified, `0/200` surviving. If any survives undetected, `F1` is not decisive and the result is reported as a failure."*

`F1`'s clean case is *"the 40 endpoints of the 20 registered `(p, eta)` worlds"*, and those endpoints are `lambda_low = lambda*/2`, `lambda_high = 3·lambda*/2` (`gmi-833-heldout-20-transitions-v1/HELDOUT_TRANSITION_FORMALIZATION_V1.md` §4: *"changes only persistent-state price from `lambda_low=lambda*/2` to `lambda_high=3lambda*/2`"*).

A shifted law with ratio `r` predicts `PERSISTENT` iff `lambda < r·lambda*`. At the low endpoint it agrees with truth iff `r > 1/2`; at the high endpoint iff `r < 3/2`. **Therefore every `r ∈ (1/2, 3/2) \ {1}` reproduces the true prediction on all 40 endpoints and cannot be caught by `F1`.** The planted positive `2·lambda*` sits at `r = 2`, outside the blind interval.

So exactly one of two things is true, and both are damaging:
- the ladder can emit `r ∈ (1/2, 3/2)`, in which case the pre-registered target `200/200` is *mathematically unachievable* and the package's own self-falsifier *"Any randomized boundary surviving `F1`"* is triggered; or
- the ladder is constructed to exclude `(1/2, 3/2)`, in which case `200/200` is a rigged null that demonstrates only that the checker catches boundaries misplaced by at least 50%, and licenses nothing about decisiveness.

The artifacts do not disclose the ladder, so I cannot say which. Either way the inference "200/200 ⇒ `F1` is decisive" is invalid.

The remedy is inside the package and was not taken: the 20 boundary worlds at `lambda = lambda*` (used by `F2`, *"all 60 registered worlds (endpoints and boundaries)"*) detect **every** `r ≠ 1`, because a shifted law predicts a strict winner where the truth is a tie. The programme owns a quantity with full power against misplaced boundaries and attached its 200-sample null to the one quantity that structurally cannot have it. I demand the ladder be disclosed and the null re-run against the boundary-inclusive quantity.

### OBJ-2 (material) — no receipt-qualifying real system could have falsified the prediction

`gmi-833-real-transition-receipts-v1/FREEZE_V2_AMENDMENT.md` §A4 derives, from the frozen grid, that *"the transition `PERSISTENT_STATE -> STATELESS` between the frozen endpoints is licensed iff `1/8 < E0 < 3/8`"*, and then: *"Systems measuring `E0` outside `(1/8, 3/8)` are `UNAVAILABLE_BY_AMENDMENT` and take their pre-registered fallback/spare; predictions apply only to licensed systems."*

The eligibility criterion is *the prediction's own sufficient condition*. §A5 adds the second gate, `persistent_beats_empirical_floor`, *"(else the system's persistent form has not demonstrated state use — the receipt fails closed)"*. So the two ways a real system could contradict the law — the price boundary falling outside the frozen `[lambda*/2, 3lambda*/2]` window, or the persistent form failing to exploit state — are respectively an exclusion criterion and a fail-closed abstention. A falsifying real system does not produce a RED receipt; it produces no receipt. That the ordering is clean (§A4: *"measured before any persistent outcome is consulted"*) protects custody but does not restore falsification risk.

This is not hypothetical. The Z15 failed-prediction register (`gmi-833-z-z15-decisive-falsifiers-v1/FREEZE_V1.md`, row `FP-KE3D`) records *"three prospectively frozen registration laws for real trained systems, each falsified by its own pre-registered falsifier; the row stays open."* Ungated real systems falsified the programme's registration laws; gated real systems went 12/12. The gate is the difference, and it is the thing under test.

### OBJ-3 (material) — the excluded systems are re-imported as confirmations

`gmi-833-real-transition-receipts-v1/CORE.md`: *"3 unlicensed systems (two ELF binaries, Front_Center.wav; measured `E0 < 1/8`) — correctly predicted NOT to transition at either endpoint: the licensed-band boundary `1/8 < E0 < 3/8` is demonstrated on both sides by real data."*

This directly contradicts §A4's *"predictions apply only to licensed systems."* Systems declared out of scope cannot then be counted as successful predictions; and "they did not transition" is the same arithmetic that excluded them, not a second, independent test.

### OBJ-4 (material) — "family-blind" classification receives the morphology variable itself

`CORE.md` line 7 advertises *"opaque-ID selection; family-blind binomial-banded classification"*. §A3 of the amendment states the classifier's inputs: *"the selected candidate's `(B, err_imm, N_imm, err_delay, N_delay)`"*, with *"`STATELESS` iff `B == 0` … `PERSISTENT_STATE` iff `B > 0` and …"*. `B` is the state-bit count — the morphology under prediction. Withholding the *name* while supplying the *variable* is not blinding. The same objection applies to the Z15 restatement's *"the search itself never sees an architecture name"* when the frozen objective is `J = eta·[…] + lambda·state_bits`: the architecture-relevant coordinate is a term in the objective.

### OBJ-5 (material) — the deployed threshold does not visibly instantiate the law it cites

`CORE.md` cites *"the #901 law `lambda* = eta*p/(2B)`"*; the amendment freezes *"`lambda* = p/(16H)`"* with `B_persist = 4H`. With `eta = 1` and `B = 4H`, `eta·p/(2B) = p/(8H)`, a factor of two from the frozen value. The frozen value instead corresponds to a stateless error of `1/4` rather than the `1/2` the #901 derivation supplies. No convention reconciling the two appears in any supplied artifact, so a reader cannot verify that the real-system package is testing the #901 law at all rather than a differently-scaled constant.

### OBJ-6 (material) — "V1 predictions unchanged" survives a retracted premise

`CORE.md` step 3: *"registered revival: empirical stateless floor, amended blind classification, exact licensed band; V1 predictions unchanged."* But §A5 says of V1's floor: *"the analytic-uniform expectation `1/2` is recorded but NOT asserted on real data (V1's assertion is retracted by this amendment)."* The numeric grid `lambda_low/lambda*/lambda_high` was *derived from* the retracted premise. Keeping the numbers while discarding the derivation means the real systems are being tested against the law's output for a hypothetical uniform-bit system, not against the law's output for themselves. That is a different, weaker claim than "the law predicts real transitions".

### OBJ-7 (material) — "resource-bounded search" is not what the mathematics is about

Z15 freeze, §"The flagship theory": *"the theory predicts which morphology a resource-bounded search will select … The prediction is made from the task's parameters alone, before any search is run."* The mathematics is the argmin of a linear, state-penalized objective over a fixed 65,552-element set. Nothing in it concerns search dynamics, budgets, or bounded resources; *any* exact optimizer returns the same answer, which is precisely why the two "materially independent routes" must agree. The prose attributes to a *search* a result that is a property of an *objective*.

### OBJ-8 (material) — 20/20 and 40/40 are one verified fact used forty times

`HELDOUT_TRANSITION_FORMALIZATION_V1.md` §4: *"The receipt records 20/20 transition predictions and 40/40 endpoint property predictions."* The enumeration independently confirms two numbers — `J0 = eta·p/2` and `J1 = lambda`. ## Answers to the brief's questions, in short

- **Precision.** C1's finite statement is precise and correct; its *prose* ("resource-bounded search", "never sees an architecture name", "on real data", "family-blind") is not. C2's statements are precise, including their hypotheses — this package is the best-quantified of the four. C3's AG1-4 is under-quantified to the point of falsity (OBJ-17) and AG1-5's Statement/Consequence are unconditional where its Assumptions are conditional. C5's A5/A7/A14 are imprecise in ways that flatter the corpus.
- **Validity as written.** I found no invalid proof in C1's derivation or in CIP-1…CIP-4, and I found the AG1 registry structurally as described. The invalidity is in inference from evidence to claim: OBJ-1 (null ⇒ decisiveness), OBJ-2 (gated receipts ⇒ real-system validation), OBJ-8 (40 arithmetic consequences ⇒ 40 tests), OBJ-18/-19/-20 (lint ⇒ descent result).
- **Does the prose claim more than the mathematics shows.** Yes, in C1, C3 and C5. Least so in C2, which is the only package here that states its own parent's defect precisely — and then mis-describes that defect's extent (OBJ-13).
- **Are the falsifiers real falsifiers.** `F1` is real but blind on `(1/2, 3/2)`. `F2` is `F1` extended. `F4` cannot fire on a correct implementation. `F3` has no lower bound on its power. `persistent_beats_empirical_floor` and `licensed_band` are not falsifiers at all — they are the prediction's own sufficient conditions used as entry criteria. CIP-1's registered falsifier (*"an admissible triple in 0 or ≥2 classes"*) is provably unsatisfiable, which is correct for a formal result but means instance-checking tests the code, not the theorem.
