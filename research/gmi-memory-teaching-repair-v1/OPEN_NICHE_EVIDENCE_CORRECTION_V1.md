## ONR-2: a sharp analytic boundary, with its premises

Suppose every admissible machine has capability c<=1 and the margin rule is
c-b>=1/24 for a known exact baseline b. Then b>23/24 makes admission impossible.
This follows by subtracting b from the universal upper bound. At b=23/24,
c=1 meets the margin exactly, provided that such a machine is attained and meets
all other criteria (including any separate theta threshold).
The strict boundary cannot be replaced by >= without losing this positive case.

E_open4 displays c=0.9688 and b=0.9271. Their displayed difference is 0.0417.
Under an explicitly assumed nearest-four-decimal rounding envelope, the true
difference lies within [0.0416,0.0418], which straddles 1/24.
The decimal display therefore does not certify exact equality to one unit.
This envelope is a sensitivity calculation, not an authenticated error model.

## ONR-3: attained samples are not family ceilings

For a supplied finite fully covered family, exhaustive maximum is attained and
can serve as an upper bound. Alternatively an analytic certificate c(M)<=u for
every admitted M supplies a family bound. A maximum over retained samples only
bounds those samples. An abstract partial register with sampled maximum 9/10
can admit an additional capability-1 member without changing an old record.
This is a compatible-family countermodel; no native implementation is supplied.

Even a true supremum equal to an admission threshold is not enough for admission
unless attained. For b=1/2 and capabilities b-1/n (n>=3), the supremum is b but
no candidate meets >=b; all these capabilities lie in the normalized domain.
By contrast a certified universal strict upper bound below the required
threshold establishes exclusion. MSC's coverage/attainment and EFI's compatible
world distinctions are the matched parents, not a new family-selection law.

The successor proposal to replace mean ceilings by attained maxima repairs the
mean-versus-robustness confusion only at the sampled set. It does not by itself
certify all-family occupancy, acquisition reachability, or global impossibility.
No new learner observations are introduced here.
