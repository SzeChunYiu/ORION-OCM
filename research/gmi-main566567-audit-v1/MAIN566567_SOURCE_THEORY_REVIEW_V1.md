## XGAC-1 — factorization accepted, novelty not established

The five-field declaration groups the existing eight-field master tuple:
I=(P,B,Theta), O=(Omega,epsilon), with E/rho/D retained. It is a vocabulary
factorization and scope discipline, not a new representation, selection,
physical accounting, acquisition or universal-completion theorem.

The document explicitly declines logical independence of its primitives and
does not claim empirical truth from the axiom names. Its trace/resource rules
remain conditional contracts; unobserved cost is not filled with zero.
The fifteen-gap ledger is a proposed programme with narrower attained scopes,
not evidence that existing Q1–Q11 obligations have disappeared.

## XGAC-2 — GAC1–4 accepted at their stated statistical scope

The predictable weighted sum of globally conditionally valid nonnegative
factors has conditional expectation at most1. Multiplying current capital
by that factor preserves nonnegative supermartingale validity. Countable
nonnegative sums use conditional monotone convergence. Ville then controls
one global threshold crossing over unbounded discrete time.

These claims need neither row independence nor fixed stopping time. They
still require the conditional bound relative to the complete global past
under every law in the declared common null. This does not turn private-row
validity into global validity, a fixed-look statistic into a valid factor,
or evidence against a common null into separate automatically certified
module claims.

GAC4 separately combines conditional whole-sequence coverage failures with
a pathwise summable error allocation. Tower expectation plus countable union
is valid. Dynamic births do not establish an alphabet, stationarity, sampler
freshness or a learned state model. In formalization, unborn rows can simply
have allocation0 and failure event empty; birth-order indices and sums must
be measurable. No power, termination, finite expected work or infinite-horizon
control-performance theorem follows.

The five existing tests give finite algebraic controls for selection leakage,
retroactive maxima, a predictable mixture, repeated full-alpha spending and
a truncated geometric allocation. They explicitly do not prove the analytic
theorem and do not test a general composition implementation.

## XGAC-3 — GAC5 needs a well-founded dependency premise

The claim invokes topological induction for an "acyclic wiring" without
stating finite or well-founded dependency depth. Countably many modules are
allowed elsewhere in this document. Mere absence of directed cycles does not
supply induction base cases.

Countermodel at the usual assume-guarantee interface reading: take modules
indexed n=0,1,..., each copying its Boolean input to output x_n; wire output
x_(n+1) to input of n. The edge relation n+1→n has no directed cycle, but its
predecessor chains never reach a source. Every component's valid total
contract is input1⇒output1. Its assumption follows from its predecessor's
guarantee. The conjunction of all guarantees implies the proposed obligation
x_0=1. Nevertheless x_n=0 for every n satisfies every wiring equation and
local implication while violating that obligation.

The components' legal type/domain/timing scopes can all be identical. What is
missing is an externally established base assumption, not a defective local
identity implementation. If "inside proved scope" was intended to require
every local assumption already independently discharged, that stronger
interpretation must be stated; it cannot be obtained from acyclicity alone.

Minimal positive repair: require a finite DAG, or a well-founded predecessor
relation with a rank r such that r(j)<r(i) whenever i relies on j. Establish
source/base assumptions from the external premises, then apply well-founded
induction. For infinite/recurrent systems, use a separately initialized and
preserved invariant as the next paragraph already requires. Finite DAGs and
the statistical GAC1–4 results are unaffected. This is established
assume-guarantee/induction discipline, not a new composition mechanism.
