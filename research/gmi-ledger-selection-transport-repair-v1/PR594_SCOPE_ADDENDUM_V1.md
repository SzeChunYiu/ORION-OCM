## What improved, and what remains scoped

The new generic-price argument is a valid sufficient separation of the
distinct operation-count vectors in the stipulated finite menu. Uniform
prices produced many ties; distinct generic charges can remove them. This
does not supply absent O0–O3 applicability or identify distinct algorithms:
Bayes and entropy mirror updates can coincide. Ties remain legitimate under
actual equal resource costs; breaking them is not an empirical discovery.

TL6/7 adds valid strict interval separation and the mirror direction.
For two nonempty closed intervals, possibly unbounded above, interpreted as
their FULL Cartesian product, every completion has A<B iff A's finite
upper endpoint is below B's lower endpoint. Necessity follows by choosing
the endpoints (or a sufficiently large A when it is unbounded).
For costs multiplied by one common positive scale, order is preserved.
Neither fact covers new allocations or changes the original infimum error.

The pinned compare function omits the validation performed by its siblings:
compare((2,1),(3,4)) with exact rational entries returns strict-lower despite
A being empty. The correction validates all endpoints before any verdict.
The valid ((1,2),(3,4)) no-alarm case still certifies strict-lower.

DCR-3 calls interval separation sufficient and explicitly disclaims complete
opaque-implementation comparison. PR594's comment that its iff is reused
verbatim from DCR is too strong; the iff requires the full-product semantics.

## Constructive comparison preserving correlations

For a supplied nonempty FINITE joint feasible set W of pairs (a,b), compute
d_min=min_W(a-b) and d_max=max_W(a-b). Then every world has a<b iff d_max<0,
and every world has a>b iff d_min>0. The finite extrema are attained, so
this is immediate in both directions. All other cases remain unresolved
as one universal strict ordering; ties are preserved.

For W={(1,2),(2,3)}, d_max=-1, so A<B in every supplied world, although its
marginal boxes [1,2] and [2,3] touch and permit a tie in their product.
This is a positive correlation-aware revival, already anticipated by EFI.
It does not identify the world's actual physical feasibility or family coverage.

The same maximum test applies on a nonempty compact W with continuous
difference; compactness supplies attainment. Without attainment, strict
pointwise order need not have a uniform positive margin:
W={(0,1/n):n>=1} always has A<B but sup(a-b)=0. The finite helper is not an
oracle for arbitrary infinite joint constraints.

## K2 overlap is a savings criterion, not reachability or a root cause

PR594's finite unit-charge model gives
|P\L|<|P| iff P intersects L. This exact saving criterion is retained.
But P=L={a} is an atomic target with history charge 0 versus RESET charge 1.
Its test saying “any library” enumerates libraries excluding the chosen
atomic target, silently imposing disjointness. If “new atomic target” means
a not in L, no savings follows at that scope; atomicity alone is insufficient.
In this model every missing part remains acquirable for unit cost, so
nonoverlap affects cost and never makes the finite target unreachable.

The original test helper itself reproduces the atomic counterexample.
No value of |P| alone identifies the cause of #323's 2/9 aggregate outcome.
Matching this abstraction to that experiment remains an unproved premise.

The new retry usefully demands strongest-parent subtraction, but its outcome
table still says majority despite declaring the old threshold unchanged.
The two-thirds correction and existing PARENT_GF_D4 result remain necessary.
A same-grammar operator retry and SECOND_GRAMMAR terminal are different
protocols; identify the operator, exact gate, grammar and fresh evidence in
one new consistent registration before execution. No retry is run here.
