# Facilitated variation / PowerPlay parent map to OCM v1

Status: **strong parent subtraction for “history makes novel future cognition easier.”**

## Why this parent matters

A broad OCM/Track-B developmental slogan is:

> experience leaves persistent structure that makes future, genuinely new tasks easier even when their exact solutions were not stored.

That phenomenon has strong predecessors.

## Facilitated variation

Parter, Kashtan & Alon (2008) evolved logic circuits and RNA structures under systematically varying environments built from recurring subgoals. Their simulated organisms:

- stored information about past environments in their genomes;
- developed modular/weakly linked structures;
- adapted rapidly to **novel combinations** of past subgoals;
- in the logic-circuit experiments, could also adapt faster when one module/subgoal had not appeared before;
- lost the advantage on unrelated/random goals outside the learned modular “language”.

Primary source:

- Parter, Kashtan & Alon, **Facilitated Variation: How Evolution Learns from Past Environments To Generalize to New Environments**, PLoS Computational Biology 4(11), 2008, DOI `10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000206`, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2563028/

This is already a direct example of:

```text
past structured experience
-> changed future variation/search geometry
-> faster adaptation to unseen but structurally related goals
```

## OOPS / PowerPlay

PowerPlay searches for new tasks together with modifications of the current solver while retaining previous capabilities. Its search is complexity-biased, can reuse previous skills, and can explicitly reward solver modifications that make previously learned tasks faster/cheaper.

Primary source:

- Schmidhuber, **PowerPlay: Training an Increasingly General Problem Solver by Continually Searching for the Simplest Still Unsolvable Problem**, Frontiers in Psychology 4:313, 2013, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3675324/

PowerPlay explicitly discusses:

```text
stored experience changing conditional search complexity
partial reuse of previous skills
new solver modifications
retention of earlier tasks
space/time efficiency as improvement objectives
```

OOPS/universal-search parents also make the search bias conditional on previously found programs/experience.

## What this kills

ORION/Track B gets no broad novelty credit for merely demonstrating:

```text
history is retained
history changes future search bias
a new target is solved faster
the exact new solution was not stored
old subskills/fragments are reused
```

Those ingredients occur in facilitated-variation, OOPS/PowerPlay, library learning/meta-learning and related fields.
