## 2. External parents, with citations

Bayesian inference, belief updating and the posterior-as-evidence-ratio are not
novel here and are not claimed to be. Neither is the descriptor-closure
construction, the dominance test, or the charged-cost model.

- T. Bayes (1763), "An Essay towards solving a Problem in the Doctrine of
  Chances", *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London*
  53:370–418. The posterior as a ratio of the accumulated evidence for a
  hypothesis to the total evidence — the mechanism this row's registered label
  instantiates, and the reason the label is *storable*: if the store holds the
  evidence, it holds the posterior.
- P.-S. Laplace (1812), *Théorie analytique des probabilités*; and (1814),
  *Essai philosophique sur les probabilités*. The inverse-probability form of
  the same update, and the first systematic use of evidence accumulation over
  a hypothesis set.
- J. Pearl (1988), *Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems: Networks of
  Plausible Inference*, Morgan Kaufmann. Belief as a distribution over
  hypotheses, updated by evidence — the class this row names.
- E. T. Jaynes (2003), *Probability Theory: The Logic of Science*, Cambridge
  University Press. The maximum-entropy and evidence-accumulation reading of
  the posterior, and the discipline that a posterior is a statement about a
  hypothesis set, not about a stored table's own contents.
- D. E. Knuth (1997), *The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 2*, 3rd ed.,
  Addison-Wesley. The multiplicative-hash permutation used as the registered
  presentation lever.
- G. Salton, A. Wong and C. S. Yang (1975), "A vector space model for automatic
  indexing", *Communications of the ACM* 18(11):613–620.
  DOI `10.1145/361219.361220`. Descriptor-term indexing over a document
  vocabulary; the descriptor-closure vocabulary is the same construction on a
  word list.

## 3. What is NOT claimed novel

- No Bayesian method, no posterior, no belief-update rule here is new.
- "A context whose completions concentrate their length in one long word
  satisfies a dominance test" is a property of the corpus, not a discovery.
- The readout language, the winner rule, the post-hoc classifier, the
  charged-cost model and the eleven-coordinate ledger are the parents' forms,
  re-implemented here rather than imported.

## 4. The residual contribution of this tranche

Two things, both methodological and small:

1. **A measured boundary with its correction record.** On the registered
   amended ecology `F17` at `SIGMA_H17R`, the intended
   `WEIGHTED_EVIDENCE_BELIEF` class loses to the store's own posterior read
   (`STORED_LABEL_READ_WITH_FALLBACK`), 11,421 held errors against 1,005, with
   the registered weight-reassignment null separating them by only 1.23×
   against the registered 3× bar while every raw count arm stays bit-identical.
   The first-drafted claim that the F1 coordinate was structurally
   unsatisfiable at this label is refuted by the package's own measurement
   (full-source optimum 0 against a half-majority of 5,049), and the refutation
   is recorded with its number and its verdict flag rather than dropped.
2. **The adjacent scoped positive that the boundary implies**, delivered
   labelled with the class actually recovered and explicitly not claimed for
   this row: on the two-evidence subpopulation (75,618 of 97,018 held queries)
   the winner rule recovers a class with all four stages agreeing and clears
   F1.

Taken together these separate, by measurement rather than by relabelling, the
sibling classes on the same sha-bound source: the fan-out count
(`EXT>=K`, `CUE_ASSOCIATION_FANOUT` at `SIGMA_H06R`), the store's own label read
(`STORED_LABEL_READ_WITH_FALLBACK`), and the intended static two-evidence
posterior over independent evidence counts (`WEIGHTED_EVIDENCE_BELIEF`) — the
last of which does **not** win here, which is why the row is left open.
