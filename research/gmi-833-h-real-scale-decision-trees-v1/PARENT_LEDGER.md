## 2. External parents, with citations

Decision trees / rule systems / conjunctive concept learning are not novel
here and are not claimed to be. Neither is the descriptor-closure
construction, the threshold-conjunction hypothesis class, or the charged-cost
model.

- J. R. Quinlan (1986), "Induction of Decision Trees", *Machine Learning*
  1(1):81–106. DOI `10.1007/BF00116251`; and J. R. Quinlan (1993), *C4.5:
  Programs for Machine Learning*, Morgan Kaufmann. The decision tree as a
  hierarchy of attribute tests; a query is classified by evaluating a
  conjunction of threshold tests against its own attributes. This is the
  family's own mechanism that this package reasons about as a readout to be
  recovered family-blind.
- L. Breiman, J. H. Friedman, R. A. Olshen and C. J. Stone (1984),
  *Classification and Regression Trees*, Wadsworth. CART: recursive binary
  partitioning by threshold tests on attributes. The threshold-conjunction
  decision rule is the CART-style rule evaluated at one node-depth.
- E. B. Hunt, J. Marin and P. J. Stone (1966), *Experiments in Induction*,
  Academic Press. CLS: concept learning as a search over rule/concept
  descriptions — the historical root of the decision-tree and rule-learning
  line.
- T. M. Mitchell (1977/1982), "Version spaces: a candidate elimination
  approach to rule learning" and "Generalization as search", *Artificial
  Intelligence* 18(2):203–226. DOI `10.1016/0004-3702(82)90040-6`. Conjunctive
  hypotheses as the version-space hypothesis class: a concept is a
  conjunction of attribute tests. The recovered `LEN<=9&CNT>=1` is exactly a
  conjunctive hypothesis of that form.
- J. S. Bruner, J. J. Goodnow and G. A. Austin (1956), *A Study of Thinking*,
  Wiley. Conjunctive concepts as the basic human concept-learning form;
  "conjunctive" is the canonical named class the finite-scope steer carries.
- D. E. Knuth (1997), *The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 2*, 3rd ed.,
  Addison-Wesley. The multiplicative-hash permutation used as the registered
  presentation lever.
- G. Salton, A. Wong and C. S. Yang (1975), "A vector space model for
  automatic indexing", *Communications of the ACM* 18(11):613–620.
  DOI `10.1145/361219.361220`. Descriptor-term indexing over a document
  vocabulary; the descriptor-closure vocabulary is the same construction on a
  word list.

## 3. What is NOT claimed novel

- No decision-tree, rule-learning, or conjunctive-concept method here is new.
- "A short descriptor that recurs across the vocabulary is a strong
  threshold-conjunction feature" is a property of the corpus, not a discovery.
- The readout language, the recovery procedure, the post-hoc classifier, the
  charged-cost model and the eleven-coordinate ledger are the parents' forms,
  re-implemented here rather than imported.

## 4. The residual contribution of this tranche

One thing, methodological and small: **the family-blind recovery of the
threshold-conjunction readout `LEN<=9&CNT>=1` (a decision rule over stored
context) as the winner of a registered recovery over a closed, pre-outcome
readout language that includes the siblings' membership and association
readouts, at real scale, with a matched-presentation negative control and a
matched membership-shuffle design null** — and the demonstration that the
recovered class (`THRESHOLD_CONJUNCTION`) is distinct from the siblings'
(`STORED_EXEMPLAR_MEMBERSHIP`, `CUE_ASSOCIATION_FANOUT`) on the same source:
a rule system tests a conjunction of conditions (length AND stored-context
membership), which neither sibling's winner does. The three neighboring
Section-H rows are therefore separated by their readouts, not by relabeling.
