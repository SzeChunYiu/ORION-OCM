    Derive skill chunking and why hierarchies are shallow (checklist item 10)
    
    Joins two proved results without assuming anything new. PVR-3 gives the reuse break-even,
    retention winning exactly when r exceeds 1 + S/(C-U) and never when invoking costs as much
    as deriving. The quotient theorem says the coarsest response-preserving quotient is the
    unique minimal representation, so a skill is a retained sub-quotient and chunking is PVR-3
    applied to it.
    
    The derived consequence is that levels are self-limiting. Retaining level one lowers the
    derivation cost of level two, but C sits in the denominator of the threshold, so lowering
    it raises the recurrence a level-two chunk must clear. Each level is harder to justify than
    the one beneath it, and the first level should capture most of the benefit.
    
    That is a prediction and the evidence confirms it. An exact enumeration over five weighted
    tasks gives flat 72, best one level 33, best two levels 31: the second level adds 2 against
    the first level's 39, because short chunks are shared across every task while long ones are
    task-specific. A sweep over storage price and recurrence shows the second level's
    contribution rising with recurrence at every price, remaining a 10 to 20 percent minority
    across the whole range, and falling to exactly zero at high storage price with low
    recurrence.
    
    States what is not settled: subgoal formation, which connects to item 11 but is not proved
    here, and hierarchical planning, since this compiles recurring structure rather than
    searching futures. Load-bearing assumptions are PVR-3's own, and eviction would reinforce
    rather than weaken the shallowness conclusion.
