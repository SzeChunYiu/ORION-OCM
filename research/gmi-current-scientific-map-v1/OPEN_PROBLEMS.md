# Open scientific problems and decisive next steps

The following are claim-specific gaps. Their closure requires an earned result
at the stated scope; adding more examples inside a supplied grammar does not
settle a broader claim.

| Claim to establish | What is missing | Smallest decisive next step |
|---|---|---|
| Reliable deployment from learned model data | Supplied state, support and conditional row laws can be wrong; certificate availability is distinct from coverage. | Fix one task/interface family, compare with a state-aliased parent, identify the missing history variable, and test its effect on adequate success and complete acquisition/deployment cost. |
| Architecture properties predicted beyond authored cases | Current mechanisms use explicit Boolean, affine, memory or factor contracts. Parameter/graph holdouts do not identify an unseen mechanism family. | Freeze property definitions and a complete candidate/comparator contract before an independent party supplies a genuinely held-out family. Score prediction, ties, abstention and all discovery costs without relabelling afterward. |
| Template-free recovery of known mechanism properties | Current executable cases do not establish every requested named family. Family names or macros can supply the answer through the grammar. | Freeze a low-level grammar, encoding and resource contract with no named-family labels or macros in search; predict properties first, then generate and execute candidates, retaining all ties and uncovered residue. |
| A computationally useful general mechanism selector | Certificate and optimizer existence are often supplied; finite exhaustive construction can be expensive. | Preserve a matched exact small-instance solver and introduce one proof-preserving pruning/composition rule; charge certificate construction and search, verify no adequate optimum or tied property fiber is lost, then test a disjoint structural family. |
| Quantitative selection at physical scale | Mathematical charges have no automatically valid hardware calibration; a shared bound does not order machines. | For one execution interface, bind measured component costs and error intervals, prove target-total accounting coverage, and compare adequate implementations with acquisition, storage, runtime and lifecycle work charged. |
| Higher-order developmental capital | Earlier behavioral history effects do not establish cheaper acquisition of new reusable capital beyond guided-first alternatives. | Register a successor grammar and genuinely new acquisition target, keep the two-thirds criterion and guided-first comparator, and test the isolated recombination mechanism with pre-solution mediation and complete costs. |
| Causal ecology-dependent emergence | Descriptor prevalence mixes proposal supply, ancestry, task mixture and selection. | Retain the entire proposal/parent/task/selection denominator; intervene on one usable mechanism at matched state and cost, include sham/restoration and compare against a matched neutral-supply control. |
| A scoped new-mechanism separation | Candidate-relative nonreduction does not exclude every possible representation or escape an unrestricted programmatic class. | Declare a complete comparator/reduction class, prove resource-preserving separation within it, and test the constructed adequate mechanism against its strongest admitted parent. This is a scoped milestone, not unrestricted novelty. |
| A complete domain basis | No finite list identifies an unrestricted physical/interface universe. Individual separations do not establish coverage. | Declare the physical/interface universe and establish complete coverage by uniformly composable reductions preserving the relevant cost, precision and adequacy contracts. Explicitly retain every uncovered sector. |

[Current results](CORE.md) already prove useful specializations. These open
claims should reuse those mechanisms and improve one failed stage at a time.
The experiments must preserve independent evaluation and their original
falsifying conditions; a conditional success needs a specified extension path.
