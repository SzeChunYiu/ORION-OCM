    Derive the concept-formation trigger and category boundary (item 9 / I2)
    
    Both halves were already proved separately and never joined. The quotient theorem gives the
    boundary: two experiences are the same concept iff they license the same future responses,
    and the coarsest such quotient is the unique minimal representation. PVR-3 gives the
    trigger: naming costs S once and saves C-U per use, so it pays iff the class recurs beyond
    1 + S/(C-U). A concept is therefore a retained equivalence class.
    
    Exact witness over fifteen experiences: three classes clear the threshold and are named,
    three singletons are declined. No concepts costs 75, the triggered set 48, and naming every
    class 57.
    
    That last figure is the result. Naming everything costs 9 more than the triggered set, so
    the law actively excludes abstraction rather than merely permitting it where it helps.
    Over-abstraction is penalised by exactly the storage spent on classes that never recur.
    Boundary verified exactly: all within-class pairs license identical responses and all
    cross-class pairs differ.
    
    An instrument correction is recorded: the first run had every class clearing the threshold,
    so selectivity was untested and the name-everything comparison came out identical. Three
    one-off experiences were added. This is the second witness in this lane needing that fix.
    
    Scope: concept naming in any linguistic sense is not derived, since this is retention of an
    equivalence class with no link to a symbol. That would run through the compositional-language
    theorem.
    
    PVR-3 now governs five cognitive phenomena -- consolidation, chunking, teaching, culture and
    concept formation -- differing only in what plays the reuse count.

    Derive subgoal decomposition after falsifying the obvious version (item 11, partial)
    
    Item 10's skill derivation suggested that a subgoal is the entry state of a retained skill.
    Complete enumeration over 30 prefix-states falsified it: 20 states show a cost-to-go drop
    against only 10 entry states, with entry states a strict subset. The extra ten have no
    applicable skill but benefit from one later in the plan, so the drop propagates backward by
    ordinary dynamic programming. "Where cost-to-go drops" over-counts subgoals two to one and
    measures reachability of a benefit rather than a decomposition point.
    
    The correction is that the benefit must be realised locally rather than inherited: a state
    is a subgoal iff its drop exceeds the drop inherited from its successor. That set is exactly
    the entry states, 10 and 10 with empty symmetric difference in both directions.
    
    The falsification is kept because the naive version is the one a reader reaches first and is
    wrong by a factor of two here, which makes the locality condition load-bearing rather than a
    technical refinement.
    
    Scope: subgoal decomposition is derived given a fixed skill set. Goal formation and the
    planning stopping rule remain open, since the acquisition theorem stops probing rather than
    search over futures. The load-bearing assumption yields a substantive prediction rather than
    a caveat: subgoals are determined by an agent's skills, so two agents with different skills
    decompose the same task differently.
