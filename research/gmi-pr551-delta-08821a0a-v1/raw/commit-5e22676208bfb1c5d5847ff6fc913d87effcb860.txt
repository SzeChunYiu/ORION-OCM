Family x ecology matrix: the flat-ceiling hypothesis fails, and memory is the broadly admissible family

The learner map suggested a predictive law -- each family has a roughly ecology-independent
ceiling, so occupancy is decided by the constant rather than the family, and species
occupancy would follow from two numbers.

Tested across six hand-built families and six ecologies. The law predicts every family's
standard deviation falls clearly below the constant's 0.0516. Two of six do. For
soft_retrieval, exemplar_table and constant_emitter, capability varies as much as or more
than the constant, so occupancy cannot be predicted that way. Recorded as a negative
because it was my hypothesis and it is wrong: family capability is genuinely
ecology-dependent, the same lesson DU-1 teaches about reachability.

constant_emitter's large spread is a sanity check that the measurement behaves, since it
is one fixed constant while the best constant is chosen per ecology.

What the data does show is memory dominance with a number attached. hamming_knn_k3 is
admissible on three of six ecologies and carries the highest mean of any row at 0.8524,
above the mean best constant of 0.8090, while soft_retrieval manages one and the rest none.

Also noted: the two gradient_net rows here are admissible nowhere while the parametrised
gradient_net(h=3, lr=1) is admissible on E_wit1 at +3.751 fx, so the learner family is
configuration-sensitive as well as ecology-sensitive -- a further reason the flat-ceiling
reading was too simple.
