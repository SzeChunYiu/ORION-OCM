Correct the memory-dominance claim: it was measured on a cost-biased subset

The admissibility ranking excluded program_search, compiled_search and particles_p4
because they were slow, and I then concluded which family is most broadly admissible from
what remained. That is a selection artifact, and a cost-correlated one.

Earlier in this same campaign I diagnosed why those rows are slow: SEARCH's budget spans
16 to 2401, selection prefers the largest because a bigger inner budget finds better
programs, and the ledger charges every inner candidate. Cost and capability are positively
coupled in exactly those rows, so excluding by cost biases toward finding cheaper weaker
families dominant, which is the conclusion I reached.

The partial full-matrix run shows it. compiled_search, minimum over six interventions, is
admissible on all four ecologies measured so far with margins from 1.5 to 4.0 fx, starting
at 0.9375 where the best cheap row averaged 0.8524. This matches the class-rate record
independently, where program_search was rule-36 admissible on E_cr1 to 4 at 3.5 fx or more.

Corrected statement: the exact-search family is the broadly admissible one, and the memory
claim should be read as broadest among the cheap rows, which is not an interesting
category. The hamming_knn_k3 numbers stand; the ranking drawn from them does not.

Two lessons kept, both about method: do not drop measurements by cost when cost correlates
with the quantity being measured, which I had already established here and walked into an
hour later; and the flat-ceiling negative is unaffected, since it compares each family's
variability against the constant row by row.
