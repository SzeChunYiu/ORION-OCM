## 2. External parents, with citations

Associative / content-addressable memory is not novel here and is not claimed
to be. Neither is the descriptor-closure construction, the trie fan-out, or
the charged-cost model.

- J. A. Anderson (1972), "A simple neural network generating an interactive
  memory", *Mathematical Biosciences* 14(3-4):197–220.
  DOI `10.1016/0025-5564(72)90075-2`. The linear associator: a stored matrix
  whose rows/columns are cross-correlations, so a partial cue reconstructs a
  stored pattern. The retrieved response is a function of the content AT the
  cue, not of the cue's addressability.
- D. J. Willshaw, O. P. Buneman and H. C. Longuet-Higgins (1969),
  "Non-holographic associative memory", *Nature* 222:960–962.
  DOI `10.1038/222960a0`. The binary associative net; one-shot storage and
  content-addressed recall through coincident activity. The trie fan-out of
  this package is a deterministic analogue: the letters stored after a cue ARE
  the associated content at the cue.
- J. J. Hopfield (1982), "Neural networks and physical systems with emergent
  collective computational abilities", *Proceedings of the National Academy
  of Sciences* 79(8):2554–2558. DOI `10.1073/pnas.79.8.2554`. Content-
  addressable memory as energy basins: a cue that is a partial/noisy version
  of the stored content settles into the matching basin. What distinguishes
  associative retrieval from indexed lookup is precisely that the cue is
  content, not an external key.
- T. Kohonen (1972), "Correlation matrix memories", *IEEE Transactions on
  Computers* C-21(4):353–359. DOI `10.1109/TC.1972.5008975`. Correlation-matrix
  associative memory and its error-correcting recall.
- P. Kanerva (1988), *Sparse Distributed Memory*, MIT Press. Content-addressable
  memory over high-dimensional random addresses; distributed associations with
  no external key.
- D. E. Knuth (1997), *The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 2*, 3rd ed.,
  Addison-Wesley. The multiplicative-hash permutation used as the registered
  presentation lever.
- G. Salton, A. Wong and C. S. Yang (1975), "A vector space model for
  automatic indexing", *Communications of the ACM* 18(11):613–620.
  DOI `10.1145/361219.361220`. Descriptor-term indexing over a document
  vocabulary; the descriptor-closure vocabulary is the same construction on a
  word list.

## 3. What is NOT claimed novel

- No memory method here is new. No associative table, no correlation matrix,
  no trie fan-out, no content-addressable recall is new.
- "A cue with more distinct stored continuations is commoner among shared
  (multi-word) descriptors" is a property of the corpus, not a discovery.
- The readout language, the recovery procedure, the post-hoc classifier, the
  charged-cost model and the eleven-coordinate ledger are the parents' forms,
  re-implemented here rather than imported.

## 4. The residual contribution of this tranche

One thing, methodological and small: **the family-blind recovery of the
cue-association fan-out readout as the winner of a registered recovery over a
closed, pre-outcome readout language, at real scale, with a
matched-presentation negative control that makes the registered presentation
lever load-bearing and a matched association-shuffle design null that
destroys exactly the content-to-content adjacency** — and the demonstration
that the recovered class (`CUE_ASSOCIATION_FANOUT`) is distinct from the
sibling's (`STORED_EXEMPLAR_MEMBERSHIP`) on the same source, so the two
neighboring Section-H rows are separated by their readouts, not by relabeling.
