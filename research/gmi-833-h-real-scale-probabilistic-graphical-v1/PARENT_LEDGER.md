## 2. External parents, with citations

Probabilistic graphical models, factorisations, factor graphs and
message-passing inference are not novel here and are not claimed to be.
Neither is the descriptor-closure construction, the fan-out statistic, or the
charged-cost model.

- J. Pearl (1988), *Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems: Networks
  of Plausible Inference*, Morgan Kaufmann. Bayesian networks: a joint
  distribution factored over a graph, with the graph encoding which variables
  are coupled. The factorisation, not the marginal, is the stored object.
- F. R. Kschischang, B. J. Frey and H.-A. Loeliger (2001), "Factor graphs and
  the sum-product algorithm", *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory*
  47(2):498–519. DOI `10.1109/18.910572`. The factor graph: a bipartite graph
  of variables and FACTORS, over which marginalisation is carried out by
  message passing between factor nodes. The recovered readout is exactly the
  product structure of that factorisation evaluated at a query.
- S. L. Lauritzen and D. J. Spiegelhalter (1988), "Local computations with
  probabilities on graphical structures and their application to expert
  systems", *JRSS B* 50(2):157–224. Local computation over a graph structure;
  the answer to a query is assembled from factor-local evidence.
- C. M. Bishop (2006), *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning*,
  Springer, ch. 8. The factor-graph formalism and its use of a product of
  factor-local terms as the joint.
- D. Koller and N. Friedman (2009), *Probabilistic Graphical Models:
  Principles and Techniques*, MIT Press. Independence structure, factors, and
  the fact that a graphical model's stored object is a product of factors
  rather than a table over the joint.
- D. E. Knuth (1997), *The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 2*, 3rd ed.,
  Addison-Wesley. The multiplicative-hash permutation used as the registered
  presentation lever.
- G. Salton, A. Wong and C. S. Yang (1975), "A vector space model for
  automatic indexing", *Communications of the ACM* 18(11):613–620.
  DOI `10.1145/361219.361220`. Descriptor-term indexing over a document
  vocabulary; the descriptor-closure vocabulary is the same construction on a
  word list.
- The in-repository parents of section 1, for the ledger shape, the classifier
  idea, the presentation lever, the null constructions and the custody chain.

## 3. What is NOT claimed novel

- No graphical-model, factor-graph or message-passing method here is new.
- "A descriptor whose continuation is attested in every population that
  contains it is a jointly-attested descriptor" is a property of the corpus,
  not a discovery.
- The readout language, the winner rule, the post-hoc classifier, the
  charged-cost model, the eleven-coordinate ledger, the null constructions and
  the control forms are the parents' forms, re-implemented here rather than
  imported.

## 4. The residual contribution of this tranche

One thing, methodological and small: **the family-blind recovery of the
factor-product (message-passing) readout `R1ASSOC>=1&R2ASSOC>=1` as the winner
of a registered recovery over a closed, pre-outcome readout language, on a
stored FACTOR GRAPH with two independent factors over disjoint token
populations, at real scale** — together with the two matched negatives that
pin the class down: a one-factor design null that destroys exactly ONE of the
two factors at matched size (the intact factor's own arm keeps its
uncorrupted error count exactly, the corrupted factor's arm falls toward
chance) and a single-factor ecology control on which the joint arm LOSES to
the single-factor arm by 13.8×. The recovered class
(`FACTOR_JOINT_CONSISTENCY`) is therefore distinct from the siblings'
(`THRESHOLD_CONJUNCTION` over one stored dimension; `STORED_EXEMPLAR_*`;
`CUE_ASSOCIATION_*`) on the same source, and the exact-inference readout over
the stored factorisation is decision-identical to the family-blind winner at
the held stage.
