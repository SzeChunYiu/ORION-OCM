# 1. Final programme question

The strongest target is:

> **Can a small externally governed cognitive machine convert verified experience and inherited knowledge into persistent reusable cognitive structure such that useful accumulated competence progressively lowers the information, search, diagnosis, revision and self-change required for later cognition at matched capability and complete lifetime cost?**

This is not a claim that:

- OCM is AGI;
- OCM will universally beat Transformers;
- symbolic systems are universally superior;
- OCM must rediscover all existing algorithms itself;
- every domain will show amortized benefit;
- every learned component must be novel;
- self-modification may rewrite the epistemic constitution;
- lower parameter count alone implies efficiency.

A complete negative or conditional result is a valid programme closure.

# 21. Definition of success

One persistent externally governed machine increasingly builds cognition from its own verified experience and inherited knowledge, later related cognition becomes cheaper, relevant cognition remains local, revision remains exact, and the heterogeneous lifetime Pareto frontier survives strongest parent products.

### Outcome B — conditional phase law

OCM-style persistence is beneficial only under identifiable regimes such as sufficiently long reuse horizon, adequate self-identifiability, sufficiently local dependency structure, manageable repair/search space and bounded maintenance.

### Outcome C — parent-product sufficiency

The complete signature is reproducible by a strongest conventional/neural/persistent parent product, making Machine Epistemics primarily a useful integration/evaluation framework rather than a distinct architecture residual.

### Outcome D — negative architectural result

Maintenance, state size, verification, diagnosis, coupling, capability loss or transfer failure prevents the predicted regime.

All four are scientifically valid programme completions.
