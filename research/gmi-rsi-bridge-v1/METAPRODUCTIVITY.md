# Metaproductivity under GMI resource accounting

## Why current performance is insufficient

An agent can be strong now yet a poor ancestor, or temporarily weak yet generate much better descendants.  The bridge therefore separates:

1. **task quality of the present machine**; and
2. **quality/resource profile of descendants produced by its developmental process**.

This absorbs the metaproductivity-performance distinction made explicit by Huxley–Gödel Machine while keeping GMI’s resource-profile discipline.

## Primary object: descendant profile

For a registered root `X`, horizon `k`, ecology `E`, and budget vector `B`, record the set/profile

\[
P_k(X;B,E)=\{(q_i,c_i,d_i,f_i,\ldots): i\in descendants_{1:k}\},
\]

where the axes are preregistered task quality and complete resource/failure observables.  Pareto comparison is preferred when no justified scalar utility exists.

## Secondary scalar

If a study preregisters a utility `U`, it may report

\[
\mu_k(X;B,E)=\frac{\max_{i\le k}U(i;E)-U(root;E)}{cost(B)}.
\]

The synthetic microscope fixes `U = shadow_quality` and a simple additive cost **only to test the evidence protocol**.  This does not override Grand GMI’s requirement to keep architecture/resource comparisons explicit.

## Causal meta-gain

For matched roots:

\[
\Delta_{meta}=\mu_k(X_{mutable-D})-\mu_k(X_{frozen-D}).
\]

A positive value can support R3 only if:

- the resource vectors match;
- the ecology/evaluation is held out from change selection as registered;
- the shadow evaluator is protected;
- `D` actually changed and descendants execute the changed `D`;
- no unregistered human change is inserted into the mutable arm.

## Horizon sensitivity

Metaproductivity is horizon-conditioned.  A study must report `k` and should report sensitivity across preregistered horizons when feasible.  Selecting `k` after seeing which candidate wins invalidates the causal claim unless corrected by a registered selection protocol.

## Stepping stones

Using best validated descendants permits a candidate to pass through a temporary regression.  This is intentional and addresses developmental reachability: a path to a superior morphology need not monotonically increase present performance at every intermediate node.

The counterweight is full receipt reporting: failures, rollbacks, resource use and negative transfer cannot be discarded just because one later descendant is strong.
